3.1

Scope & Independence

This document is scoped entirely to the Flagship tier as a standalone building — a different site, different water features, different boundary from Said-ul-Ajaib in every respect. Where Document 0 marked the Flagship tier "aspirational, not yet built," this is that tier's actual architectural template.

Formerly scoped as "Interior & Fit-Out Design" in Document 0's index — renamed here to Architecture & Site Design, since what follows is full building form and landscape, not interior finishing. Embedded Anchor and Kiosk tiers will get their own, more constrained sections in a later revision of this document.

3.2

Orientation Principle

Flexible — Adapts by Context

Front / Right / Left / Back throughout this document are relative feature labels, not compass directions. Which way the building actually faces at a given site — and how these zones respond to sun path, prevailing wind, and monsoon exposure — is a per-site decision made under Document 0 §0.7's climate guardrails. Nothing here fixes a real-world orientation.

Zone Breakdown

01

Front

Ref: Workstation Coffee-style glass facade

Full-glass facade and entrance, in the dramatic angled-glass-and-signage manner of the Workstation Coffee reference — the building announces itself from the street rather than hiding behind a conventional shopfront.

Entrance threshold only — no seating counted here; folds into Main Hall's footprint in the sizing table below.

02

Right Extension — Open-Roof Strolling Water Garden Not Frontyard / Backyard

Ref: arched windows, stone loggia with reflecting pool, floating flowers

Separated from the main hall by full floor-to-ceiling arched windows. Beyond them, an open-roof outdoor strolling area in the manner of a stone arcade beside a long reflecting pool, with seasonal floating flower arrangements on the water surface. A third outdoor zone in its own right — not part of the frontyard or backyard.

~17 seats · ~610 sq ft, including the reflecting pool and loggia walkway (mid-covers scenario — see sizing table below)

03

Left Extension — Enclosed Glass Conservatory Not Frontyard / Backyard

Ref: curved glass conservatory roof, hanging greenery

Reached through a sliding door from the main hall, opening into a fully enclosed space under a curved glass roof — hanging plant life overhead, warm pendant lighting below. Deliberately not a mirror of the Right Extension: enclosed where the right is open-air, a conservatory mood rather than a garden-loggia one.

~20 seats · ~460 sq ft (mid-covers scenario — see sizing table below)

04

Main Hall

Double-height ceiling

The counter sits in direct sightline from the entrance. Side seating runs in rows along the walls — richer, jewel-toned banquette seating in places, drum-pendant-lit rows elsewhere. At the center, leaving clear walking space on all sides: a real indoor pond with a single small ornamental tree planted at its center, surrounded by several family-size seating pods that read as floating platforms — each carrying its own small winding moss-and-stone table surface. A stone walkway with lantern-topped plinths circles the whole feature at the perimeter.

~45 seats · ~1,650 sq ft, including the pond, walkway, counter and entry threshold (mid-covers scenario — see sizing table below)

Engineering approach — fixed structure, reads as floating

Built as fixed pods with glass tank walls below and around the seating, not genuinely buoyant platforms. This is the actual construction behind Baoding's Koi Pond Restaurant (Food Town, Hebei, China), which achieves the identical "surrounded by fish and water" sensation this way — round tables sit within colossal glass aquarium tanks, so diners feel immersed without ever touching water or standing on anything that moves. Same effect you're after, far simpler and safer engineering: structural glazing and aquarium filtration are mature, standard fields — marine floating-platform engineering is not. Each pod's tabletop can still genuinely float small serving trays across its own shallow channel, in the spirit of Singapore's Incloud teahouse — lightweight service theater, not load-bearing.

05

Back Extension — Two Sections

Overlooks the backyard

Jaali + Drape Lounge: a terracotta jaali screen ceiling layer — patterned light and shade rather than a solid soffit — paired with flowing fabric drapery in the manner of a jungle-canopy lounge. Lantern + Rose Terrace: overhead lanterns under a creeping rose canopy, positioned to look directly out over the backyard.

~27 seats combined · ~750 sq ft, split roughly evenly between the two sub-sections (mid-covers scenario — see sizing table below)

06

Kitchen Back-of-House

Placement: top-right corner, behind Back Extension

Combined bakery-production and full hot-line kitchen — the two build-outs Document 2 §2.9 treats as distinct, sharing one footprint at Café-Restaurant stage. Sits against an exterior wall for exhaust and suppression access, with its own service entrance separate from the guest glass-facade entry, and short travel distance to the Main Hall counter for service flow.

~1,330 sq ft (mid-covers scenario — see sizing table below). Ventilation and suppression design is Document 12's, not this document's — §3.9 flags this as a direct dependency.

07

Washrooms & Back-of-House Back-of-House

Guest washrooms (separate Ladies, Gents, and one accessible/universal WC), reached via a short corridor branching off the existing Main Hall-to-Back-Extension route rather than cutting through any guest experience zone. Staff washroom, dry storage, and a small manager's office share this block, kept deliberately away from the Left and Right Extensions' guest-facing character.

3.5

Lighting & Ceiling Language

Document 0 §0.3 locks the ring-form-plus-greenery fixture as the one non-negotiable signature. Here's how it's expressed across zones that call for different moods:

  • Main Hall & both extensions: the ring/drum pendant form is the primary fixture language — clustered overhead installations, greenery worked through them.
  • Back Extension, rose terrace specifically: traditional overhead lanterns take the lead instead, as a warm garden-party accent — a deliberate, secondary departure from the ring form, used only in this one zone.
  • Counter, main hall: a green, shredded-fringe curtain treatment overhead, functioning almost as a soft, textured canopy rather than a fixture — distinct from the ring language, reserved for this one spot to mark the counter as a destination within the hall.

Every one of these stays inside Document 0's warm-light-only rule — no exceptions, in any zone.

3.6

Water Systems — Two Distinct Ecologies

Two separate water systems, deliberately built to different logic:

Right Extension — Garden Pond

  • Still, clear water
  • Submerged plants only — nothing breaks the surface
  • Small fish, kept for water clarity and mosquito control
  • Floating flower trays as seasonal decoration

Main Hall — Central Pond

  • Structural glass tank construction — seating pods are fixed, not buoyant
  • One small ornamental tree planted at the center, on its own fixed plinth
  • Emergent plants — visibly breaking the surface
  • Larger, more visible fish, safely sealed behind glass
3.7

Boundary, Frontyard & Backyard

A low-rise wall — tall enough to mark the property line clearly, short enough that it never hides the building itself. Backed by trees, privacy bushes, and creepers, deliberately mixing native species with ornamental ones: the native planting supports local pollinators and wildlife, the ornamental layer carries the visual softness, and together they buffer the property from street noise without turning the boundary into a solid, closed-off wall.

Land Area Target — For Site Search

Per §3.4's sizing table: roughly 6,000 sq ft of total built-up area at the mid-covers scenario (5,300–6,800 sq ft across the low–high range). Applying a 40% ground-coverage assumption — generous enough to leave real room for the frontyard, backyard, and boundary planting described above, tight enough to stay realistic for a South Delhi commercial plot — that implies a target plot of roughly 15,000 sq ft (≈1,670 sq yd, ≈0.35 acres), with a working range of 13,200–16,900 sq ft (1,470–1,880 sq yd, 0.30–0.39 acres) across the covers range. This is the number to search plots against, not a final figure — actual ground-coverage and FAR norms vary by the specific municipal zone once a candidate site is identified, and would need checking against that site's actual by-laws.
