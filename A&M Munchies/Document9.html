9.1

Scope & Purpose

Document 0 §0.6 originally scoped this as "fully local — starts with Said-ul-Ajaib," on the assumption regulatory compliance is a jurisdiction question, not a format one. That's half right: the actual laws are jurisdiction-specific, but which laws even apply — and how heavily — depends on format tier just as much as city. A Kiosk and a Flagship built on the same street face genuinely different compliance loads. This document uses Delhi/India law as the grounded reference framework throughout, since that's the one jurisdiction with a real site to check against; a tier built somewhere else needs its specific laws re-verified, but the tier-based reasoning here should still transfer.

Not legal advice

This document is a planning reference, compiled from current public sources — not a substitute for a lawyer or licensed compliance consultant before anything gets filed.

9.2

Regulatory Categories — Universal

Five categories apply to some degree at every tier; only their weight changes.

FSSAI

Food Safety License

Basic Registration, State License, or Central License — determined by annual turnover, not by choice. See §9.7 for a major, very recent change here.

Eating House

Delhi Police License (Sec. 112, Delhi Police Act)

Required for any place serving food or drink to the public — including takeaway-only, per the legal definition. Fee scales with floor area and seating; 3-year validity.

GST

Goods & Services Tax Registration

Mandatory above ₹20 lakh turnover for services. Every tier crosses this quickly once operating.

Fire Safety

Fire NOC or Self-Certified Site Plan

Full NOC triggers at 50+ seats or 90+ sq. meters floor area — either condition is enough on its own. Below both, a site plan with fire measures marked suffices instead.

POSH Act

Internal Committee

Already covered in full in Document 8 §8.5 — not repeated here. Triggers at 10+ employees, covers interns explicitly.

9.3

Kiosk Tier

  • FSSAI: Basic Registration — a Kiosk's turnover almost certainly sits well under the ₹1.5 crore threshold, especially early on.
  • Eating House License: genuinely ambiguous rather than clearly exempt. The legal definition covers takeaway as well as on-premises consumption, so "no seating" doesn't automatically mean "no license" — this needs a direct check against a compact, no-seating format specifically, not an assumption either way.
  • Fire NOC: very likely exempt — a Kiosk's footprint sits far under both the seat and floor-area triggers.
  • Host-venue layer: a Kiosk modeled on the HEZI HOME format (Document 0 §0.6) sits inside someone else's building — a mall, most likely. That means a second, separate compliance layer on top of municipal law: the host venue's own tenant requirements, which this document can't specify until a real host venue exists.
9.4

Embedded Anchor Tier — Said-ul-Ajaib

Said-ul-Ajaib — the first embedded-tier location, within SCRIPTORIUM — is the one real, current branch, and the most concrete section in this document.

  • FSSAI: almost certainly Basic Registration at launch, re-checked as real turnover comes in — the category is a turnover fact, not a planning choice, and belongs with Document 10 once projections exist.
  • Eating House License: required — real seating, on-premises consumption, no ambiguity here the way there is for Kiosk.
  • Fire NOC: depends on the actual floor area and seat count against the 50-seat / 90-sq.-meter dual trigger. Not resolved here — needs the real Said-ul-Ajaib floor plan, which sits outside this document's scope.
Said-ul-Ajaib's Lal Dora status

Already established as relevant to this site's construction bylaws, given its narrow-lane fire access. Worth being precise about what that status actually affects: Lal Dora classification historically eases building-plan and construction bylaws in Delhi urban villages — it does not exempt a food business from FSSAI, Eating House licensing, or fire-safety obligations, which run on separate legal tracks entirely. The narrow-lane access constraint is a physical fact fire trucks still have to contend with regardless of the site's legal classification. This is nuanced enough that it genuinely needs a local compliance consultant, not just this document, before Said-ul-Ajaib's fire NOC question gets resolved.

9.5

Flagship Tier

Template-level, since Document 3 itself calls this tier "aspirational, not yet built."

  • FSSAI: most exposed tier to crossing into State License territory, especially once Café-Restaurant stage (Document 0 §0.8) is reached — Basic Registration shouldn't be assumed the way it more safely can be for Kiosk.
  • Eating House License: required, and at the higher end of the fee scale given the building's full floor area across all four zones.
  • Fire NOC: very likely required outright — a double-height Main Hall plus four zones almost certainly clears both the 50-seat and 90-sq.-meter thresholds on the enclosed core alone.
  • DPCC Consent: a Delhi Pollution Control Committee consent letter is already a standard eating-house document requirement — worth flagging as a real line item given how much water is actually moving through this building's design (Document 3 §3.6), not just a formality to skip past.
A genuinely useful exemption, if built in Delhi

Delhi's unified open-air dining policy exempts ground-floor open space from any additional fire NOC beyond what the enclosed structure already requires, and exempts upper-floor open space under 90 sq. meters too. That maps directly onto Document 3's Right Extension and the Back Extension's rose terrace — both open-air by design. This doesn't remove the Main Hall's own fire NOC requirement, but it means those two zones likely don't add a second, separate one on top of it. This is a Delhi-specific rule; it doesn't automatically transfer if the Flagship ends up built elsewhere.

9.7

Recent Change Worth Flagging — FSSAI 2026 Amendment

This is genuinely new, not background knowledge — FSSAI's turnover thresholds were overhauled by amendment regulations notified 10 March 2026, in effect from 1 April 2026:

  • Basic Registration threshold raised from ₹12 lakh to ₹1.5 crore — a huge jump that pulls most small F&B operations, including a Kiosk and likely early Said-ul-Ajaib, out of State License territory entirely.
  • State License now covers ₹1.5 crore to ₹50 crore (up from the old ₹20 crore Central threshold) — FSSAI estimates 98% of Indian food businesses now sit in this single band.
  • Central License now only applies above ₹50 crore, or to specific categories regardless of turnover (importers, exporters, multi-state chains, central-government-premises operators).
  • Licenses now carry perpetual validity — the old 1–5 year renewal cycle is gone as of 10 March 2026.

Government fees: ₹100 (Basic), ₹2,000–5,000 (State), ₹7,500 (Central) — before any professional filing charges.
