7.1

Scope & Purpose

Three explicit dependencies land here: Document 2 §2.8's ingredient philosophy, Document 4 §4.9's fabric and footwear brief, and packaging vendors that were blocked on Document 1's finished mark. This document answers all three, plus sets what "premium" actually means as a sourcing standard rather than a marketing word.

7.2

What "Premium" Means Here

Real, never compound

Couverture chocolate, not compound. Real dairy butter, not vanaspati. Document 2 §2.8's "no cost-driven substitution" rule, made literal.

Traceable origin

Single-origin or named-estate wherever the category allows it — not anonymous bulk commodity with a premium label stuck on top.

Values, not just price

A vendor whose own story holds up next to this brand's isn't a soft nice-to-have — see §7.3.

Local-first when local wins

A Delhi-roasted coffee bean reaching Said-ul-Ajaib — the first embedded-tier location, within SCRIPTORIUM — fresher than an imported one isn't a compromise — it's the actually-premium choice. See §7.4.

7.3

Chocolate & Cacao

The Brownie Box's core ingredient, and the one place Document 2 §2.8 called non-negotiable everywhere.

Primary recommendation

Mason & Co

Puducherry / South India

Bean-to-bar, cacao sourced from organic-certified farms across Kerala, Karnataka, and Tamil Nadu.

The real reason this is the primary pick: Mason & Co's production is carried out by an all-female workforce. Sourcing from them doesn't just clear the ingredient-quality bar — it puts Document 8's entire women's-empowerment thread into the supply chain itself, not only into internal hiring. Bulk supply for restaurants and hotels is already part of their offering.

Backup / secondary

Kocoatrait

India

Organic, single-origin bean-to-bar couverture at multiple cacao percentages — a solid fallback if Mason & Co's capacity doesn't scale with demand.

7.4

Coffee

The beverage-program anchor from Document 2 §2.5.

Primary — Said-ul-Ajaib

Rossette Coffee Lab

Delhi

Specialty-grade (SCA 80+), direct-sourced from farmers, roasted in-house on a German Probat roaster. Delhi-based means genuinely shorter supply chain to this branch, not just a marketing claim — and they offer barista training and café consultation alongside supply.

National backup / future branches

Blue Tokai

Multi-city, India

India's most established specialty roaster, single-estate Arabica, small-batch roasted. The right fallback once a branch sits somewhere Rossette can't reach efficiently — the "local-first" principle in §7.2 still applies, just against a different local.

7.5

Dairy & Seasonal Fruit

Butter: real dairy butter only, everywhere, full stop — this is Document 2 §2.8's non-negotiable line, and it doesn't get a named vendor here because the principle matters more than the brand; any certified dairy supplier meeting that one bar qualifies.

Fruit: mango and strawberry for the Individual Boxed Desserts source seasonally and locally per branch, exactly as Document 2 §2.8 already set — Delhi's own mango season anchors Said-ul-Ajaib's version rather than year-round imported fruit standing in for it.

7.6

Packaging

Kraft or window box for the Brownie Box, clear acrylic for the Individual Boxed Desserts — both specified in Document 2 §2.6, both waiting on a vendor. The acrylic-box packaging you referenced at the very start of this project (the newbakekraft reference) is worth revisiting directly as a candidate lead now that Document 1's mark exists to print on it.

7.7

Uniform Fabric & Footwear

Document 4 §4.9 named this document as the block. Two specs to source against, both already fixed: fabric — lightweight cotton-linen blend for summer, heavier weight in the same cut for winter, per Document 4 §4.5's climate guardrails; footwear — non-slip sole rating is mandatory for Counter and Floor roles per Document 4 §4.6, not optional or role-dependent. Specific supplier names aren't set here — the spec is complete enough for procurement to shop it out.

7.8

Vendor Vetting Standard

Global, fixed: real ingredients only, traceable origin preferred, values-alignment weighed alongside price and consistency — never price alone. Local, flexible: the actual vendor roster differs by branch and city, following §7.2's local-first principle rather than defaulting to whichever supplier served the first location.
