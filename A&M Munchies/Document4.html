4.1

Scope & Purpose

Document 0 §0.7 already set the rule: silhouette and color are Core, fabric weight and layering are Flexible. This document defines the actual silhouette, builds a role system and a designation-badge layer on top of it so guests can tell staff apart at a glance, sets the climate-adaptation framework globally with the first embedded-tier location, within SCRIPTORIUM, Said-ul-Ajaib, worked through as the reference example, and specifies the hygiene equipment every location holds to regardless of climate or format tier.

4.2

Role System

Four roles, drawn directly from what Document 2 and Document 3 already established rather than invented fresh: the counter staff visible from the entrance, floor staff working the side seating and extensions, kitchen and bakery staff on production, and a host role for the more composed zones — the arched-window extension, the jaali lounge, the rose terrace.

4.3

Core Silhouette & Palette — Fixed

The base: a bib apron in Botanical Green over a Parchment shirt, mark embroidered at the chest. This is the Counter role's exact uniform, and the reference silhouette every other role's variation is measured against.

Counter / Barista — reference silhouette
  • Never changes across roles: Parchment as the base garment color, Botanical Green as the accent color, the mark's position on the dominant garment panel.
  • Never changes across climate: the silhouette itself — apron shape, shirt cut, role-to-garment logic.
4.4

4.5

Designation Badges

§4.4 differentiates role at the garment level — apron cut, vest, neckerchief. The designation badge sits a layer finer: a fixed-format badge worn on top of any role's uniform, carrying the individual's name and title rather than just their role category. It's the difference between "someone in a green apron" and "Rohan, Counter" at a glance — a small addition that reads as considerably more polished.

Format — Fixed

  • Engraved brushed-gold metal — the mark's existing gold, not a new material color introduced for badges
  • Two lines: first name, then role title (e.g. "ROHAN" over "Counter")
  • Same shape and engraving style across every role, and unchanged by climate zone (§4.6)

Placement by Role

  • Counter: pinned above the mark on the apron bib
  • Floor: pinned at the shirt collar or breast pocket — no bib to pin to once it's dropped for mobility
  • Kitchen / Bakery: small tag clipped to the cap band, clear of the work surface
  • Host: engraved directly into the lapel pin itself, rather than as a separate badge
Embedded Anchor — note

The Said-ul-Ajaib Embedded Anchor format will likely carry a second badge alongside the designation badge — a collaboration badge, for the co-located partnership itself, along the lines already sketched (Presented By / Co-Branded Stack / Story-Driven Tag, placed at the apron chest, sleeve, or back yoke). That spec belongs to a dedicated Embedded Anchor tier document once one exists; this document's focus is the Flagship tier, and this note is a placeholder rather than a finished design.

4.6

Climate Adaptation — Global Framework

This is the one part of the system Document 0 §0.7 marked Flexible — fabric weight and layering, never silhouette or color. Said-ul-Ajaib gave the first worked example of that rule below, but a document carrying the "Global Brand Standard" tag needs the rule stated for climate in general, not just the one site it happened to get designed against first.

The table below uses the Köppen climate classification, the standard global reference for grouping climates by temperature and precipitation pattern. Only fabric weight and layering vary by row — Parchment, Botanical Green, and the mark's placement hold across every one of them, exactly as they hold across every role in §4.3.

Köppen GroupCharacterFabric WeightLayering
A — Tropical Warm and humid year-round; little seasonal swing Lightest weight, moisture-wicking cotton-linen None — short sleeves standard across all roles, all year
B — Arid / Semi-Arid Low humidity, high daytime heat, sharp day–night swing Lightweight, breathable, tightly-woven against sun exposure Optional light layer for early-morning or evening service
C — Temperate Seasonal — mild-to-hot summers, cool-to-cold winters, often a distinct dry season Mid-weight base, swapped seasonally to a heavier weave Optional cardigan/liner layer for the cooler season
D — Continental Hot summers, genuinely cold winters — the widest seasonal swing of the realistic set Mid-weight summer fabric, heavier insulated option for winter Full layering system in winter — base shirt, cardigan, then an outer layer for any outdoor-facing role
E — Polar Cold year-round, minimal warm season Not a realistic expansion zone for this format at present — flagged rather than designed for

Said-ul-Ajaib itself sits in Group C — Cwa specifically, monsoon-influenced humid subtropical with a hot summer and a genuinely dry winter (some datasets place Delhi right at the border with BSh, semi-arid; Cwa is the standard designation and the one used here). The worked example below is that row, applied.

Worked Example — Said-ul-Ajaib (Cwa)

Summer (into the mid-40s°C)

  • Short sleeves, lightweight cotton-linen blend
  • Minimal layering — no vest over apron for floor/counter roles

Winter (cold, foggy, poor air quality)

  • Long sleeves, heavier-weight fabric, same cut
  • An optional Botanical Green cardigan layer, not a silhouette change
Monsoon

Quick-dry fabric treatment across all roles, not just outdoor-facing ones — the Right Extension and Main Hall pond mean "outdoor-facing" isn't a clean category here the way it would be at a fully enclosed branch.

4.7

Food Safety & Hygiene Equipment — Global Standard

Unlike §4.6, this layer doesn't flex by climate at all — it flexes only by how close a role gets to actual food contact. The World Health Organization estimates 600 million cases of foodborne illness occur globally each year, and of the cases traced back to food-handler behavior, an estimated 89% involve hand contact — which is the actual argument for gloves and mouth covers existing as a fixed category, not a Said-ul-Ajaib-specific add-on.

The baseline follows the Codex Alimentarius General Principles of Food Hygiene — the WHO/FAO reference point most national food-safety regulators build from — with FSSAI Schedule 4 as the statutory floor for the Said-ul-Ajaib site specifically. Every future location meets whichever of the two, Codex or the applicable local code, sets the higher bar.

All Roles

  • Hair fully enclosed — cap or hairnet, not just a tie-back
  • Trimmed, unpolished nails; no rings, watches, or loose jewellery
  • Clean uniform per §4.3, changed if visibly soiled mid-shift

Direct Food-Contact Roles (Counter, Kitchen/Bakery)

  • Mouth cover worn during plating and any ready-to-eat handling — surgical-style or a clear anti-fog spit guard, changed at least every six hours or sooner if damp
  • Single-use, food-contact-safe gloves (nitrile, not latex, to avoid allergy exposure), changed between raw and cooked handling
Not a substitute

Gloves and masks supplement handwashing; they don't replace it. Both Codex and FSSAI are explicit that glove use without proper hand hygiene underneath creates a false sense of security rather than an actual control — training on when to change gloves matters as much as the gloves themselves.

4.8

Practical & Physical Safety

  • Non-slip footwear, mandatory for any role working near the Right Extension pond or the Main Hall's central glass-tank feature (Document 3 §3.4, §3.6) — standing water and foot traffic are a real combination, not a hypothetical one.
  • Kitchen/Bakery fabrics should be heat-safe and non-synthetic near open flame or ovens — feeds directly into Document 12's fire safety planning rather than being decided in isolation here.
4.9

The Mark in Application

Every placement here follows Document 1's simplification rule directly: at this scale, the brownie renders as a solid block with no interior drizzle or chunk detail — exactly the "below 24px, simplify" guidance from Document 1 §1.6, now actually applied rather than just specified.
