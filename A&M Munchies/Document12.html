12.2

Sonic Fire Tech — Final Determination

Back when this first came up, the read was "worth tracking, not specifying" — US-only deployment, no India pathway visible. Checking again now, the answer is firmer, not softer.

Not specifiable — for a different reason than before

Sonic Fire Tech's own current messaging states interior applications remain limited to non-occupied environments, with occupied-space certification described as a "long-term vision" still pending code approval — and that's for residential use. A commercial kitchen is occupied, interior, and cooking-specific: further from certification than even the occupied-home case they're still working toward. Their actual current commercial deployment is exterior wildfire defense for homes in high-risk California communities — a genuinely different product category from a kitchen hood system. Nothing in their public materials mentions India, NBC compliance, or commercial kitchen certification of any kind.

This isn't "check back later" anymore. It's a real, specific gap between what the product does today and what an occupied commercial kitchen needs — worth remembering as a category to watch as fire-tech matures, not something to build a real kitchen's compliance around.

12.3

The Standard — What Gets Specified Instead

UL300-compliant wet chemical hood suppression, designed to NFPA 17A, integrated with NFPA 96-compliant hood/duct/plenum ventilation — the actual, current, India-available standard. Two real options:

Primary — premium tier

Ansul K-Guard / PIRANHA

Aster Technologies — authorized India dealer

The globally trusted Ansul name, certified to NBC and NFPA standards. The PIRANHA hybrid system specifically is worth naming: agent-plus-water, cools grease roughly 15× faster than single-agent wet systems in UL300 testing, with better coverage and less chemical needed. Consistent with Document 7 §7.2's "premium, not just expensive" standard — this is the better system, not just the pricier name.

Secondary / comparison

Ceasefire India

India, LPCB certified

Wet chemical system rated for Class A, B, and F (cooking oil) fires, full hood/duct/plenum coverage, in-house design support. A strong Indian-origin alternative worth quoting alongside Ansul before final selection.

Multiple Delhi-based installers exist for either system (several NCR-based suppliers found, GST-registered, 8–15 years in the trade) — genuinely local execution, not an imported-only solution.

12.4

Two Kitchen Profiles

Document 2 §2.7 already drew this line — it matters again here because the two profiles carry different fire risk, not just different equipment.

Bakery-Production (current)

  • Ovens, proofing, cold storage — no open flame cooking surface
  • Lighter suppression need, but not zero — confirm against actual equipment once specified, not assumed exempt

Full Hot-Line (Café-Restaurant stage)

  • Fryers, griddles, open flame — grease-laden vapor becomes a real factor
  • Full UL300 wet-chemical hood system genuinely mandatory here, not optional
12.5

Architecture-Specific Risk Factors

Document 3's own design choices, read for fire risk rather than aesthetics:

  • Jaali + drape lounge (Back Extension): flowing fabric near a dining zone needs fire-retardant treatment as a baseline spec, not an afterthought — this is the same fabric §3.4 already flagged as needing a proper weatherproof roof underneath, and now it needs a flame-rating spec too.
  • Green fringe curtain over the counter (Main Hall): fabric installed close to a food-service zone — same fire-retardant treatment requirement applies.
  • Double-height Main Hall: smoke behaves differently in tall spaces than in a standard single-story room — detector placement needs real engineering input, not standard single-story spacing assumptions.
  • Open-roof Right Extension: different ventilation dynamics than the enclosed zones — likely favorable for smoke dispersal, but not a substitute for proper detection where it adjoins the enclosed Main Hall.
