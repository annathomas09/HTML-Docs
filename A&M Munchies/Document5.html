5.1

Scope & Purpose

Document 0 §0.6 left this loose on purpose: "brand grammar fixed; aggregators/local social per branch." This document is where that grammar actually gets fixed — translated from "pinteresty, instagrammable, eye-appealing, smooth, classy, 5-star" into rules specific enough to check a draft post against, rather than general social-media advice that could apply to anyone.

Website and app get a direction here, not a full spec — wireframes and user flows are their own separate effort once this direction is agreed.

5.2

What "Pinteresty & Instagrammable" Means Here

Six words, translated into six checkable rules — each one tied to a decision this suite has already made, not invented fresh.

On-palette, always

Every post uses the five Document 0 colors. Nothing borrows a palette from a reference image, however good that image is — see §5.5.

Warm light, no exceptions

Document 0's rule extended to photography specifically: no cool or blue-toned product shots, ever, in any post or ad.

Negative space is the rule

"Classy" mostly means empty space used on purpose. One dominant subject per post, not a collage.

Motion is slow, not busy

"Smooth" means deliberate, unhurried transitions — the dessert-slider reference (§5.6) is the pace to match, not a fast-cut Reel style.

The mark appears once

One small, correctly-scaled placement per asset, per Document 1's own rules — never stamped repeatedly across a single image.

Real product, real texture

"5-star" reads as specificity — real drizzle, real crumb, real steam — not glossy stock-photo abstraction.

5.3

Photography & Videography Direction

The AI-prompt reference you sent is a genuinely useful technique — single dramatic light source, matte surfaces, controlled shadow, no noise — but its actual color grade runs cold (asphalt grey into asphalt blue), which breaks §5.2's warm-light rule outright. Same technique, rebuilt in this brand's own light:

Adapted prompt — Brownie Box hero shot

"A single brownie from the Assorted Brownie Box, caramel drizzle catching warm golden light from one angled source above; resting on a dark botanical-green ceramic plate against a deep charcoal backdrop; entirely matte, no harsh reflections; a single out-of-focus trailing leaf visible at the frame's edge; the shadow falls long and warm across the surface; every light source warm-toned, nothing cool or blue anywhere in frame."

The short product-footage clip you sent is the right pace and mood for video content generally — dramatic, unhurried, single-subject. Same warm-light correction applies there too.

5.4

Content Formats

Variety Showcase

The cheesecake pin's format — several versions of one product in a single graphic — is exactly what Document 2 §2.2 already built for the six Brownie Box varieties. That content is effectively ready to become a real post; it just needs A&M's palette in place of red-and-checkerboard.

Labeled Cross-Section

The matcha cake reference — a clean product photo with layer callouts, plus a small practical note about lead time — is a strong, reusable format for the Individual Boxed Desserts, and honest in a way that photographs well: it shows the product is actually made a certain way, not just styled to look like it.

5.6

Website Direction

One signature interaction, borrowed directly from the Figma reference: a slow, smooth product carousel on the homepage — the six Brownie Box varieties or a hero sequence from the Flagship's Main Hall (Document 3) sliding past at an unhurried pace, not a fast auto-rotating banner. Beyond that one moment, the site should read as this document suite already looks: dark, warm-lit, Fraunces for headlines, generous space around every image.

5.7

App Direction

One brand-wide app, not a per-branch build — a customer who knows A&M Munchies in one city should recognize it instantly in another. What's exposed inside it can still vary by branch and format tier:

  • Embedded Anchor (Said-ul-Ajaib, within SCRIPTORIUM): pre-order and pickup has real, specific value here — someone studying in a Capsule pod shouldn't have to leave it to queue at the counter.
  • Kiosk (future): app ordering is lower-priority by design — the format is built to be low-friction without it.
  • Flagship (future): full functionality, including reservations once Café-Restaurant stage is reached.
