1.1

Scope & Purpose

Document 0 §0.3 left one piece of Core Visual DNA unresolved: no finished logo or nomenclature system existed yet. This document closes that gap. Everything that touches the name in public — signage, packaging, uniforms, digital, every future branch in every format tier — inherits directly from what's defined here.

1.2

Nomenclature

  • Full name, always: "A&M Munchies" — ampersand, not "and"; both words capitalized as shown.
  • Standalone "A&M": acceptable in casual or spoken contexts. The full name appears in every formal, printed, or legal context — signage, packaging, contracts.
  • Branch locators (e.g. "— Said-ul-Ajaib," the first embedded-tier location within SCRIPTORIUM) are never merged into the wordmark itself. They sit as a separate line or tag beneath it, only where a specific branch needs identifying.
Open — needs your input

What "A&M" actually stands for isn't something I'll invent — that's yours to supply whenever you want it on record. Nothing downstream depends on the answer; the initials work as a fixed name either way.

Worth knowing before this goes further

A quick trademark check: "A&M Munchies" as a combined name doesn't turn up an existing registration. But "MUNCHIES" alone has a real history of contest in India — several standalone filings show as Objected, Refused, or Opposed, including one specifically in Class 43, which covers restaurant and food services — the exact class this business would file under. That doesn't mean "A&M Munchies" can't be registered cleanly; a distinctive combined mark is generally a stronger position than the bare word alone. But it does mean a proper clearance search through a trademark professional is worth doing before the name gets built into signage, packaging, and everything else in this document. I'm not a lawyer and this isn't legal advice — just what a search surfaces.

1.3

The Mark

Built directly from Document 0 §0.3: the ring form, reduced to logo scale, with two leaves in place of the full trailing vine — and, at the center, a brownie. A bare ring reads as generic wellness or hospitality iconography; it doesn't say "food" to someone seeing it cold. The brownie fixes that, and it's not an arbitrary bakery symbol — it's been the actual product running through this brand's reference material since the very first images you sent.

Why the ring recolors but the brownie doesn't

Dark-surface and light-surface versions swap the ring and leaves between gold and green, per Document 0's rule that gold represents light itself. The brownie stays the same realistic chocolate-and-caramel color in both versions — it's a literal object, not an abstract signature, so it doesn't get reinterpreted by context the way the ring does.

Considered and rejected

A coffee cup — the single most overused cafe icon there is. A croissant — increasingly a cliché of its own for anywhere European-leaning. A chef's hat — reads catering, not cafe. Crossed fork and knife — generic fine-dining shorthand that says nothing specific. The brownie wins over all of them for one reason: it's not a borrowed category symbol for "what bakeries usually use" — it's the actual product this brand has been built around since your first reference images.

1.4

Construction & Clear Space

Simple rule, easy to enforce across vendors who'll never read the full document: keep clear space equal to at least half the ring's radius on every side, measured from the ring's outer edge — including past the leaves, which are allowed to breach the ring itself but never the clear-space zone.

Clear-space zone, dashed — scales with the mark
1.5

Color Application

  • Dark surfaces: ring in Signature Gold, leaves in the two Document 0 greens, wordmark in Parchment.
  • Light / print surfaces: ring and leaves shift to a deeper, darker green range — gold stays reserved for the illuminated, dark-surface version only, consistent with Document 0's rule that gold represents the light form specifically.
  • Single-color contexts (embossing, one-ink printing, foil stamping): the whole mark renders in one flat color — gold, ink, or cream depending on the surface — ring and leaves undifferentiated.
  • Never: Terracotta as a primary mark color. It stays a minor accent per Document 0, not a brand color for the logo itself.
1.6

Minimum Size & Don'ts

Icon-only mark: never below 24px digital / 15mm print. Horizontal lockup: never below 120px digital wide.

Below 24px — simplify

The drizzle lines and chunk texture on the brownie won't hold at favicon scale. Below 24px, drop them: ring, leaves, and a solid brownie block with no interior detail. Never shrink the full detailed version — swap to the simplified one instead.

Stretch or distort the ring into an oval
Recolor outside the palette defined in §1.5
Add drop shadows, bevels, or 3D effects
Rotate the ring off its horizontal axis
Place on a busy background without a solid-color holding shape behind it
Bake a branch name into the wordmark itself
