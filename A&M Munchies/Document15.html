15.1

Scope & Relationship to Document 3

This document covers on-site electricity generation and the backup-power question for the Flagship building: what the roof can actually carry, how much of the connected load it realistically offsets, and what Delhi's grid and air-quality rules permit. It is not a full electrical design — panel schedules, inverter placement, cable runs, and distribution-board sizing are an MEP consultant's task once a real site exists, the same boundary Document 12 and Document 14 already drew for fire suppression and plumbing respectively.

Ground-mounted solar isn't considered here. Document 13 has already claimed the frontyard and backyard for landscape and boundary planting — competing with that for the same square footage isn't this document's call to make.

15.2

Design Philosophy

Document 0 §0.3 fixes water paired with greenery as the Flagship's one visible signature. Energy infrastructure deliberately isn't a second one — panels belong on the Kitchen and Washrooms/BOH roofs, both back-of-house zones already, not on the Left Extension's curved glass roof or the Right Extension's open-air water garden, both guest-facing design features in their own right (Document 3 §3.4). The brief here is efficiency first, generation second: LED fixtures throughout — including the ring-light signature Document 3 §3.5 applies zone by zone — right-sized refrigeration, and decent insulation reduce the load a solar system then has to chase, rather than oversizing generation to cover a load nobody examined first.

Core — Fixed Everywhere

Solar here is sized to meaningfully offset daytime baseline load — lighting, refrigeration, POS, a share of HVAC — not to run the kitchen. At this roof scale, that's an honest ceiling, not a shortfall. §15.4 and §15.5 show the math side by side rather than quietly picking one number that flatters the other.

15.6

Backup Power — Why Delhi Makes This Harder Than It Looks

Two things rule out the obvious answers. First: net-metering rules require mandatory anti-islanding protection, so a standard grid-tied solar system shuts down automatically during a power cut — §15.4's system does nothing for backup unless paired with battery storage and a hybrid inverter, a materially different and costlier spec. Second: diesel gensets, Delhi's traditional backup answer, are restricted under the Graded Response Action Plan (GRAP) during the region's worst pollution episodes — restrictions that have historically clustered in the same October–February window a restaurant most wants reliable dinner-service backup for. A genset that can't legally run on the nights AQI is worst is a real planning gap, not a theoretical one.

Flexible — Needs a Decision

Three real options, not one obvious answer: battery storage paired with the solar system (cleanest, most expensive, immune to GRAP staging); a CNG- or PNG-fuelled genset (GRAP has historically treated gas-fuelled sets more leniently than diesel, though exact terms shift stage by stage — confirm current CAQM directions before relying on this); or grid-only supply with no backup, treating outages as an operational risk to plan around rather than an engineering problem to solve. This document doesn't pick one — §15.8 flags it for a decision once real cost quotes exist.

15.7

Net Metering, Compliance & Financial Incentives

Document 3 §3.7 leaves the Flagship's real site undecided — a plot search against the ~15,000 sqft target, not a fixed address — so this section stays deliberately DISCOM-agnostic. Delhi runs three distribution companies (BSES Rajdhani, BSES Yamuna, Tata Power-DDL), but all three sit under the same Delhi Electricity Regulatory Commission (DERC) rulebook, so the policy mechanics below hold regardless of which one the eventual site lands under; only the exact tariff slab and application portal are DISCOM-specific.

Any system here is governed by the Delhi Solar Energy Policy 2023 (notified March 2024), which targets 750 MW of rooftop solar citywide by 2026–27 and applies to any system 1 kWp or larger, residential or commercial. Net metering banks daytime export against night-time draw on an annual true-up; anything beyond 90% of yearly consumption offset is treated as free energy exported to the grid — not carried forward, not paid out. Solar modules must come from MNRE's ALMM List-II as of June 2026, a real sourcing constraint rather than a formality: a non-compliant module means the net-metering application is refused outright, not merely delayed.

Commercial, Not Residential

PM Surya Ghar's subsidy is residential-only — A&M Munchies doesn't qualify. The real financial lever for a commercial system is 40% first-year accelerated depreciation on the written-down-value method under Section 32 of the Income Tax Act, plus whatever Generation-Based Incentive the current DERC schedule sets for commercial consumers. The policy makes commercial and industrial consumers eligible for GBI; the exact per-unit rate needs confirming at application, not assumed here.

Delhi's effective commercial (C&I) electricity tariff runs roughly ₹12.5–15.6 per unit across the three DISCOMs as of 2026 — the gap that makes offsetting daytime draw worth doing. A real payback calculation against installed system cost belongs in Document 10, once actual installer quotes exist, not here.
