Document 16 of 17 — Indoor Planting

Trailing, flowering greenery for Document 3's enclosed zones — the plant half of Document 0's water-paired-with-green signature, wherever a pond isn't already carrying it. The ring-light chandelier was never meant to stay empty; this is where it actually gets planted.

Flagship tier only Fourth of four new environmental documents Version 1 — Draft for review
16.1

Scope & Relationship to Document 3

This document covers potted and integrated planting inside the building envelope, in zones Document 3 hasn't already resolved with a water feature of its own. It does not touch the Right Extension's garden pond or the Main Hall's central pond planting — both already specified in Document 3 §3.6 — and it does not touch anything outside the envelope, which is Document 13's, resolved on its own species list in §13.6.

The Kitchen is out of scope entirely — food-safety zones don't carry ornamental planting, full stop. Washrooms/BOH gets one brief mention in §16.3 and nothing more; it's functional space, not a design focus.

Flexible — No Overlap With Document 13

Every species below was checked against Document 13 §13.6's outdoor list before inclusion. Where a plant already does outdoor duty there — Mogra and Raat ki Rani are both already placed near the Back Extension's outdoor terrace — it isn't repeated indoors here, even where it would otherwise fit, to keep the two lists legible as distinct rather than one list split awkwardly in half.

16.2

Design Philosophy

Document 0 §0.3 fixes the ring-light chandelier as Core, non-negotiable brand DNA: greenery "physically integrated into or trailing through it," at chandelier scale specifically for Flagship. This document is where that instruction becomes an actual plant, not a rendering. The same section fixes water paired with green as the brand's other constant — Document 3 §3.6 already built the water half in the Main Hall and Right Extension ponds; this document is the green half wherever those ponds don't already carry it on their own.

Flowering, not just foliage, is the standing brief for this palette. Foliage plants still earn their place — for structure, resilience, and the corners bright bloomers won't tolerate — but color is the point here, not an afterthought bolted on.

Core — Fixed Everywhere

The chandelier planting in Main Hall isn't optional or a later add-on. Document 0 marks the integration itself Core, meaning every Flagship, wherever it's eventually built, carries a planted ring-light. Species can vary by climate; the fact of a living chandelier doesn't.

One more filter runs under everything below that Document 0 didn't need to apply: this is a dining room, often with children in it. §16.7 explains how that shaped the list.

Left Extension — Primary

The only zone with a conventional glass roof and no existing water/planting feature of its own (Document 3 §3.3), which makes it the obvious anchor for a genuine indoor-conservatory treatment — the brightest, most flower-forward room in the building. Ranged planters along the curved glass line, mixed heights, color held all year rather than seasonal.

The same glass roof that makes this zone bright also means real solar heat gain through a Delhi summer — worth checking against Document 3's glazing spec once one exists, not assumed away here. See §16.8.

Main Hall — Supplementary, Chandelier-Led

The pond and its single ornamental tree are already Document 3's, fully resolved in §3.6 — this document doesn't touch either. What it adds is the ring-light chandelier itself: a trailing, partly-flowering planting worked into the fixture's structure at the double-height scale Document 0 calls for. A handful of floor-level accents around the hall's perimeter round this zone out without competing with the pond as the room's visual center.

Back Extension — Supplementary

Jaali-filtered, dappled light rather than direct sun — lower-light flowering species here rather than the Left Extension's bright-light ones. Kept deliberately understated next to the jaali-and-drape lounge treatment already established, rather than adding a second competing visual layer.

Washrooms/BOH: one hardy, low-light foliage accent at the corridor transition is enough — not a design focus, just enough to avoid a dead patch of hallway.

16.5

Watering & Maintenance

Indoor pots draw from mains water, not the harvested-rainwater system Document 14 built. That system is plumbed to Document 13's outdoor irrigation and the two ornamental ponds only (Document 14 §14.2) — extending it indoors would mean a second distribution line this document isn't specifying, and mixing harvested water into guest-facing indoor zones raises its own cleanliness questions this document isn't equipped to wave through.

A simple hand-watering routine covers the floor-level planting; anything above easy reach — the chandelier specifically — realistically needs either a small drip line on a timer or a scheduled staff routine with the right equipment to reach it safely. Who actually does this — housekeeping, a dedicated role, or an outsourced plant-maintenance contract — is Document 8's call, not this document's.

16.6

Light — Resolving Document 15's Open Item

Document 15 §15.9 flagged this forward: "Any supplemental grow-lighting Document 16 specifies for its flowering varieties is a load this document hasn't yet counted." Resolved here — none needed, by design. The palette in §16.4 is matched to each zone's actual light rather than applied uniformly: the Left Extension's glass roof carries the brightest bloomers (hibiscus, ixora, orchid) on natural light alone, while Main Hall and the Back Extension get the lower-light-tolerant flowering species (peace lily, anthurium, the chandelier's lipstick plant) instead of forcing sun-lovers into dimmer rooms. Document 15's electrical load estimate stands as written — no revision needed.

This assumes daylight levels typical of a glass-roofed, double-height space at Delhi's latitude — a real light-meter reading once a site exists is the actual check, not this document's estimate. Main Hall's roof type is itself still Open in Document 15 §15.3; if it turns out considerably dimmer than assumed once resolved, supplemental lighting for that zone specifically may need revisiting.

16.7

Safety Filter

This is a dining room, and family-sized seating is part of the brand plan — so every species on the list was run through one more filter before inclusion. Anything seriously toxic if ingested is off the list entirely: no Oleander, no Dieffenbachia, no Sago Palm, whatever their design appeal. A smaller group — Peace Lily, Anthurium, Croton — carries only mild irritation if chewed in real quantity, which is a real but low-severity risk common enough that most 5-star hospitality interiors carry at least one of them. These stay on the list, placed at counter height or above rather than at a child's reach, not removed outright.

If a zero-tolerance policy is preferred instead of a placement-based one, all three are easy to swap for equally on-brand safer substitutes — flagged as a real decision in §16.8, not assumed either way.
