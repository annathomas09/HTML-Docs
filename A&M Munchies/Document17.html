17.1

Purpose & scope

This document sets the material, construction, branding, and vendor-conduct standard for every physical package A&M Munchies hands a customer — across all three tiers. It sits downstream of Doc 2 (which decides what leaves the kitchen) and upstream of Doc 10 and Doc 11 (which price and model what's specified here).

Per Doc 0's brand positioning, A&M Munchies benchmarks food quality, food safety, and infrastructure at a 5-star standard throughout — not only at the Flagship. That standard extends to packaging: a Kiosk grab-and-go bag at a metro station and a Flagship gift order should read as the same brand at the same level of care, even though they carry very different cost ceilings (see §17.3).

As a reminder for anyone new to the suite: A&M Munchies is an independent global café-restaurant brand, not subordinate to SCRIPTORIUM, built around three formats — Flagship (full café-restaurant, the primary planning focus, debuting in Said-ul-Ajaib before expanding to global capitals), Kiosk (grab-and-go, airports and metro stations), and Embedded (the in-house dining wing inside SCRIPTORIUM, TAU, and EGEB Headquarters). Flagship and Kiosk earn full commercial revenue; the Embedded tier earns revenue only at its first site — the embedded-tier wing within SCRIPTORIUM, Said-ul-Ajaib — while the TAU and EGEB Headquarters locations operate as a non-revenue service wing for students and faculty. That revenue split is the reason §17.3 sets a lower packaging-cost ceiling for TAU/EGEB than for the other three contexts.

17.2

The ring seal — packaging's signature device

Every Flagship café greets a guest with the same fixed idea: a ring light haloed in greenery at the entrance. Packaging carries that idea in miniature. Every format — cup, box, tin, or pouch — closes under one small brass-foil ring seal stamped with the wordmark, sized and placed differently per substrate but never substituted with a different device.

It is the one constant across nine very different materials (see §17.4), which is deliberate: it means a Kiosk bag at the airport and a Flagship gift box read as the same brand at a glance, without needing the same box shape, colour, or price point to do it. Vendors are held to the seal's exact placement and minimum size in §17.6 — it is the single non-negotiable branding element in every purchase order.

17.5

Sustainability & materials code of conduct

Five rules, applied without exception across all three tiers:

  1. No virgin single-use plastic in any customer-facing package, cutlery included. Cutlery defaults to FSC-certified wood or is skipped unless requested.
  2. No mixed-material laminates. A cup is either fully compostable (paper + PLA lining) or fully recyclable (rPET) — never a plastic-coated paper that can't be sorted into either stream. This is the same logic that already excludes composite laminates from the sourcing conversation in Doc 7.
  3. FSC-certified paperboard is the floor, not an upgrade, for every kraft and rigid-board format.
  4. Reusable beats recyclable beats compostable beats everything else, in that order of preference, whenever the format allows it — hence glass at Flagship dine-in and the deposit-backed laddoo tin.
  5. Regulatory alignment. Every format meets FSSAI packaging and labelling requirements ahead of Doc 9's licensing pass, and the compost/recycle ratings above are cross-checked against the sustainability posture already set in Docs 13–15 (landscape, water harvesting, green energy) so the café's back-of-house practice and its packaging tell the same story.
17.6

Branding application standards

The ring seal — placement rules

  • Minimum diameter 12mm on any single-serve item, scaling up to the largest execution on gift and hamper boxes (§17.4).
  • Never resized below legibility of the "A&M" mark inside it.
  • Foil-stamped or embossed wherever the unit economics allow it (Flagship, gift, laddoo tins); printed at manufacture for Kiosk volumes.
  • Exactly one seal per package — it is a signature, not a repeating pattern.

Typography on pack

  • Fraunces — wordmark and hero product names only.
  • Karla — ingredient lists, allergen notes, any customer-facing sentence.
  • Space Mono — batch codes, dates, weights, anything numeric or regulatory.

Colour-by-family system

Rather than branding every box identically, each product family carries one accent from the core palette, so a customer can sort a mixed order by colour before reading a label:

17.7

Vendor & sourcing code of conduct

This extends Doc 7's sourcing philosophy from ingredients to substrates.

  • Local manufacture preference. Packaging vendors within reach of the Flagship's debut site in Said-ul-Ajaib, South Delhi, are preferred over long-haul suppliers, both to cut transport footprint and to support Delhi-NCR packaging MSMEs.
  • Ethical labour audit. Every packaging vendor is audited to the same labour standard applied to food suppliers in Doc 7 before a purchase order is placed, repeated annually.
  • Small-batch tooling for the Brownie Box. Six varieties sharing one box format means the insert tooling, not the box itself, carries the SKU complexity — vendors must support short-run insert changes without re-tooling the outer box.
  • No exclusivity lock-in. At least two qualified vendors per material category at all times, so a single supplier failure never stops production.
  • Quality-control checkpoint. Ring-seal placement, board weight, and compostability certification are spot-checked on every incoming batch, not just at vendor onboarding.

17.9

Visual reference direction

The four silhouettes in Fig. 17-A, and the material choices through §17.4, were directed against a shared image library rather than invented from nothing — the packaging-systems category first indexed across this project's earlier batches (kraft bags, handled paper boxes, clamshells, tins, edible cups, tote-bag carriers, ribbon gift boxes, cocktail-to-go caddies), plus a newer, more specific set: domed sealed cups with printed branding and sticker seals, and individually wrapped, stick-format packs.

Why the reference photos aren't embedded directly

The Drive folder is genuinely useful as a private mood board, but it's a flat, unsorted export of raw phone screenshots — Pinterest and Xiaohongshu app chrome, other people's usernames and comments, and in a few cases a competitor's own trademark and packaging design, still in frame. Reproducing those directly in a document meant to go to contractors, architects, or investors would look unfinished at best and borrow someone else's branding at worst. So the standard through this suite holds: real image or a written description — and here, "real image" means an original illustration built in-house from the reference, in A&M Munchies' own palette and mark, not a re-hosted screenshot.

If a specific reference photo would help a vendor conversation more than the line art does — for instance, showing a manufacturer the exact dome-lid profile — the right move is to open that one image from the Drive folder directly in that conversation, not paste it into this document.
