8.2

People Philosophy — "Compassion for Every Being"

Worth being specific about what the Tata comparison actually means, rather than just invoking the name. Tata's version of this isn't a slogan either — it traces to the Zoroastrian ethical triad the founding family were raised on, humata, hukhta, hvarshta: good thoughts, good words, good deeds. It's expressed structurally, not just rhetorically — over two-thirds of Tata Sons is held by Tata Trusts specifically so that profit channels back into social welfare rather than only to shareholders. And it's stated almost word-for-word by Tata's own hospitality arm: IHCL, which runs the Taj hotels, says employee wellbeing there is "safeguarded... crafted with compassion."

A&M Munchies doesn't have Tata's scale or trust structure. But "every being" is deliberately wider than "every employee" — it's the same ethic already built into Document 3's water systems, where the extension pond and the Main Hall tank are engineered on genuinely different logic because the life in each deserves different care, not identical treatment. A people philosophy that stopped at "be nice to staff" would be narrower than what this brand already does for its fish. The internship program below is this document's version of Tata Trusts' structural instinct: when profit comes, some of it is built to flow back out, on purpose, not left to discretion.

8.4

Women's Empowerment Internship Program

Proposed name: The Bloom Internship — open to rename.

  • Open to: students in culinary, hospitality, or related fields — priority given to female applicants specifically, not merely "equal consideration."
  • Structure: a fixed term rotating across Counter, Floor, and Kitchen/Bakery, so every intern leaves with real range, not one narrow task repeated.
  • Compensation: paid, set above the typical Indian F&B internship norm — where unpaid or token-stipend placements are still common — as a deliberate choice, not a baseline. Exact figure belongs in Document 10 once real budgets exist; the principle doesn't wait for that number.
  • Mentorship: every intern is paired with a senior staff member; senior female staff are prioritized as mentors for female interns specifically, wherever one is available.
  • Certification: a basic food-safety credential as a portable, resume-real takeaway — not just "experience," something an intern can point to afterward.
  • Safety: no unsupervised late solo shifts for interns; transport support for any shift ending after dark; direct, known access to the Internal Committee in §8.5.
  • Pathway: strong performers get priority consideration for a real Staff-level offer at the end of term.
8.5

Legal Foundation — POSH Act & the Internal Committee

Given §8.4's explicit focus, this isn't background reading — it's the structure that makes the empowerment claim real rather than aspirational. The Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act, 2013 requires any workplace with 10 or more people — and this explicitly includes interns, not only full-time staff — to constitute an Internal Committee.

Presiding Officer

A senior woman employee, chairs the committee

Internal Member

Employee with legal, HR, or social-work grounding

Internal Member

Same criteria — at least 50% of the IC must be women

External Member

From an NGO or with direct expertise in the issue — mandatory, not optional

Real, common failure — worth naming

A 2024 survey of senior HR professionals found 59% of companies hadn't constituted a legally compliant Internal Committee at all — most failures are the external-member seat left empty or unqualified, which courts have ruled invalidates the committee entirely. Non-compliance itself carries a fine up to ₹50,000, doubling on repeat.

Recommendation — ahead of the legal trigger

Kiosk-tier and early Said-ul-Ajaib headcount may sit under the 10-person threshold where an IC isn't yet legally required. Given §8.2's actual claim, waiting for the threshold to force the issue would be the wrong instinct — voluntary early adoption is the more consistent choice, and it's cheap relative to what it protects.

Statutory bonus — the floor, not the plan

The Payment of Bonus Act applies once an establishment carries 20 or more employees, and requires a minimum 8.33% annual bonus for anyone earning ₹21,000/month or less in basic + DA. 8.9

Working Hours, Leave, Holidays & Festival Bonuses

Both tiers sit under the Delhi Shops and Establishments Act, 1954, which currently caps work at 9 hours a day and 48 hours a week, requires a break after no more than 5 continuous hours, and sets overtime at twice the normal rate — not 1.5×; that figure appears in the SCRIPTORIUM staff plan and is worth revisiting there, since Section 8 of the Act itself specifies double time, confirmed directly against the Delhi Labour Department's own published text.

A law mid-change — worth tracking, not yet acting on

The Delhi Shops and Establishments (Amendment) Act, 2026 was passed and gazetted on 11 March 2026, but had not been brought into force as of this writing — it takes effect only on a separate commencement date the Delhi government has not yet notified. Until then, the original 1954 terms above govern. Once commenced, the amendment raises the daily cap to 10 hours (inclusive of breaks), raises the continuous-work limit to 6 hours, and — most relevant here — narrows the Act's applicability to establishments with 20 or more employees, which would technically exempt both Kiosk and, most of the time, Flagship. A&M Munchies' policy below is written to the stricter, currently-active standard, which also happens to satisfy the looser one that's coming — so nothing here needs to change when the amendment commences. Confirm current status with a labour law consultant before the first hire, the same standing instruction SCRIPTORIUM's own document already carries.

Operating hours & shifts
  • Kiosk: 7:30am–9:00pm. A single overlapping pair of shifts covers this without anyone approaching the 9-hour cap.
  • Flagship: 8:00am–11:00pm, breakfast through late dessert service. Two 9-hour shifts (opening 7:30am–4:30pm, closing 2:00pm–11:00pm) with a 2–4:30pm overlap for handover.
  • No one works more than 6 consecutive days without a day off, at either tier.
  • Rosters published at least two weeks ahead; changed only with 48 hours' notice barring a genuine emergency — the same standard SCRIPTORIUM already holds itself to.
Annual leave
  • Earned Leave: 18 days/year — 3 above the Delhi S&E Act's 15-day floor
  • Casual Leave: 8 days/year, per the Act
  • Sick Leave: 12 days/year; a medical certificate is asked for only after 3 consecutive days
  • National & Restricted Holidays: 14/year, per Central Government notification
  • Total: 52 days off a year — roughly 1 day in 7, identical to the standard already set for SCRIPTORIUM, arrived at independently for the same reason

Festival bonuses — every festival, every person, no exceptions

§8.2 already made the case for why "every being" is the operative word, not "every employee." The same reasoning applies here in payroll form: no one is asked their religion, and no one's bonus depends on the answer. A Hindu staff member receives an Eid bonus; a Muslim staff member receives a Diwali bonus. The Payment of Bonus Act sets a statutory floor of 8.33% of annual salary for staff earning under ₹21,000/month basic — A&M Munchies treats that as the floor, not the plan, and extends the same structure to Kiosk-tier staff voluntarily, ahead of the 20-employee threshold that would otherwise make it optional, on the same logic §8.5 already applies to the Internal Committee.

8.10

How to Hire Them — Recruitment Channels & Hiring Process

§8.7 already covers where to look for each role. This section covers what §8.7 doesn't: who actually conducts the hiring, and what a candidate goes through once they've applied.

Who hires, without a Campus Director to lean on

Embedded Anchor benefits from SCRIPTORIUM's Campus Director handling Tier 2 and Tier 3 hiring, per that document's own §09a. Kiosk and Flagship have no equivalent layer — they're standalone A&M Munchies sites. Until a second standalone site exists to justify a dedicated regional hire, Anna hires every Branch Manager and every Kiosk Lead directly, the same way she hires every Tier 1 role at SCRIPTORIUM. Branch Managers and Kiosk Leads then hire their own Staff-level teams once confirmed — they know the role better than a founder hiring at a distance ever could.

Process by seniority

Not every hire carries the same risk. A wrong Staff-level hire is correctable inside a trial period; a wrong Branch Manager or Kiosk Lead runs an entire site badly for months before it's obvious. The process scales accordingly:

01
All roles, all tiers

Structured interview

Every candidate, at every level, gets a real conversation before anything else: relevant experience, a role-specific knowledge check (for Counter — walk me through the menu as if I'm a first-time visitor; for Kitchen or Bakery — what would you change about this recipe and why; for a Branch Manager or Kiosk Lead candidate — how would you apply §8.2's philosophy on a bad shift, not a good one), and a direct read on whether they've actually engaged with what A&M Munchies is, not just that they want a job.

02
All roles, all tiers

Paid practical trial

A half-day, paid at the relevant hourly rate. Counter candidates work a real queue; Kitchen and Bakery candidates cook or bake under observation — Bakery's trial runs across more than one batch specifically to test the consistency §8.7 flags as its one hard dealbreaker; Branch Manager and Kiosk Lead candidates run a full shift with real authority over it, not a shadow shift. This stage is never skipped, on the same principle SCRIPTORIUM's own hiring process already states: what someone does in half a day reveals more than any interview answer.

03
Senior/Lead, Kiosk Lead, Branch Manager only

References — two, by phone

Direct managers only, not colleagues. Three questions, every time: would you hire them again and why; what were they like on a bad day; what would they need to improve. A reference with no honest qualification anywhere in the answer is a flag, not a reassurance.

04
All roles, all tiers

90-day probation, reviewed at Day 30 and Day 60

Day 30 asks whether the physical systems have been learned — the till, the machines, the recipes. Day 60 asks whether decisions are being made independently or still deferred upward. Day 90 is confirmation. For a Branch Manager or Kiosk Lead, Anna conducts the Day 90 review personally, mirroring the standard SCRIPTORIUM already holds for its own Tier 1 hires.

Counter, Floor, Kitchen, and Bakery Staff go through Stages 1, 2, and 4. Senior/Lead, Kiosk Lead, and Branch Manager go through all four.
