01 Library Furniture — SCRIPTORIUM Reads ₹25 – 40 L

The library is the identity-defining room of the campus. Every furniture decision here is a brand decision. No plastic, no laminate, no flatpack. The materials are dark walnut or teak for case goods, dark leather for seating, wrought iron for metalwork, and brass for hardware. These are the materials specified in the Architecture Brief (Document 02) and they are non-negotiable — substituting them for cheaper alternatives undermines the Gothic collegiate aesthetic that generates the organic photography described in Document 07.

04 Cyber Café Hardware — SCRIPTORIUM Studio ₹18 – 26 L

Twenty stations, all at the same specification. The minimum acceptable spec for a premium study campus in 2026 is RTX 4060 / 144Hz. The target spec from the cost estimation is RTX 4070-tier / 165Hz. Do not mix specifications across stations — a single lower-spec station becomes the station nobody books. All hardware to be sourced from Nehru Place, New Delhi — India's largest IT market and the correct venue for this volume of custom builds.

Lead times for custom items are long. Commission custom items first.

08 Glossary of Terms

This document uses financial, trade, and technical terms that may be unfamiliar to first-time operators or anyone reading the planning package without a background in procurement, construction, or hardware. Every term defined below appears somewhere in Documents 03, 05, 06, 07, or 08. Definitions are written for practical use — what the term means in the specific context of building and running SCRIPTORIUM — not as dictionary entries.

Financial Terms
Working Capital
The cash a business needs to cover its day-to-day running costs before it starts earning enough revenue to pay those costs itself. Think of it as the fuel in the tank before the engine starts generating its own power. For SCRIPTORIUM, working capital covers staff salaries, utilities, marketing, and consumables during the first 6–8 months of operation — the period when the campus is open but not yet at break-even revenue. Working capital is not spent on the building or the fixtures; it is spent on keeping the business running while it builds its audience. The revised figure for SCRIPTORIUM is ₹1.1 – 1.4 crore, held as a liquid reserve (bank overdraft facility or cash in a current account), not locked into assets. See Document 05 (Business Plan) for the full break-even model.
Capital ExpenditureCapEx
Money spent on physical assets that the business will use for years — the building, the fit-out, the furniture, the espresso machine, the cyber café computers. CapEx is a one-time (or infrequent) investment, not a recurring cost. In this document, everything in Sections 01 through 05 (library furniture, stationery fixtures, café equipment, cyber café hardware, and the opening stationery inventory fixtures) is CapEx. The distinction matters for accounting: CapEx is recorded as an asset on the balance sheet and depreciated over its useful life, rather than expensed immediately. For SCRIPTORIUM, total CapEx is estimated at ₹20.5 – 28 crore (Scenario B, own plot) per Document 03.
Operating ExpenditureOpEx
The recurring costs of running the business every month — staff salaries, electricity, internet, cleaning, marketing, CA fees, consumables. OpEx is what the business pays to stay open regardless of how much revenue it earns that month. SCRIPTORIUM's Year 1 OpEx is ₹127.9 lakh annually per the revised figures in Document 05. The relationship between OpEx and revenue determines the break-even point: when monthly revenue × contribution margin exceeds monthly OpEx, the campus covers its own costs.
MRPMaximum Retail Price
The price printed on a product's packaging, set by the manufacturer — the highest price at which it can legally be sold to a consumer in India under the Legal Metrology Act. In procurement, MRP is the ceiling, not the target. Trade accounts, wholesale purchases, and bulk orders are all negotiated below MRP. Throughout this document, discounts of 20–50% below MRP are referenced for books, stationery, and hardware — these are realistic figures for institutional buyers purchasing in volume.
GSTGoods and Services Tax
India's unified indirect tax, applicable to most goods and services purchased for business use. As a registered business, SCRIPTORIUM can claim input tax credit (ITC) on GST paid on most purchases — meaning the GST paid on furniture, equipment, and stationery inventory can be offset against the GST collected on sales. Books are exempt from GST under the current regime. Stationery, café equipment, and hardware attract GST at 12–18% — all invoices from vendors must include a GSTIN for ITC to be claimable. Always ask vendors for a GST invoice, not just a receipt. See Document 01 (Regulatory Dossier) for the full GST registration and compliance framework.
Contingency
A budget reserve set aside for costs that were not anticipated at the time of planning. In construction and procurement, things always cost more than the initial estimate — a fabricator quotes ₹80,000 for a rolling ladder and invoices ₹95,000 after a design change; a book order arrives with 10% of titles out of stock; the espresso machine requires an additional electrical circuit that was not in the original MEP design. The standard contingency for a project at this stage is 15–20% of the total budget. For SCRIPTORIUM, the cost estimation (Document 03) builds in a contingency line — do not treat it as spare money that can be reallocated to upgrades. It will be spent.
Trade Account
A formal purchasing relationship between a retailer and a supplier or distributor, established in advance of orders, that gives the retailer access to wholesale pricing (below MRP), credit terms (typically 30–60 days to pay after invoice), and priority stock allocation. Throughout this document, "open a trade account with" means contacting the supplier's sales or accounts team, submitting a business registration document (GST certificate, Udyam/MSME registration), and agreeing to a minimum order value per cycle. A trade account with Faber-Castell India, for example, gives SCRIPTORIUM Ink access to their full catalogue at 30–40% below MRP — this margin is what makes the stationery floor commercially viable.
Wholesale
Buying goods in bulk directly from a manufacturer, distributor, or wholesale market at prices significantly below retail MRP. The wholesale markets referenced in this document — Nai Sarak (books), Kirti Nagar (furniture), Chandni Chowk (stationery and hardware), Nehru Place (IT hardware), Lajpat Rai Market (electrical) — are physical marketplaces where distributors and agents sell to retailers and businesses at trade pricing. Wholesale purchasing requires buying in minimum quantities (per publisher, per brand, per SKU) and paying upfront or on short credit terms. The savings over retail purchasing at the volumes SCRIPTORIUM requires are substantial — typically 25–50%.
Distributor
A company that buys products from a manufacturer and sells them to retailers within a defined territory. In India, most international brands (Lamy, Faber-Castell, Rhodia, La Marzocco) do not sell directly to individual retailers — they appoint one or two national distributors who then supply retail accounts. Identifying and opening an account with the correct Indian distributor is the first step for most of the stationery and café equipment purchasing in this document. Distributors offer better pricing than buying from a retailer, but require minimum order quantities and advance registration.
Procurement & Construction Terms
Bespoke
Custom-made to a specific brief, as opposed to bought off the shelf. In this document, bespoke items are those that do not exist as standard products and must be designed, specified, and fabricated from scratch — the rolling library ladders, the Pen Wall back-panel, the mezzanine railings, the staircase. Bespoke fabrication requires: a written brief (ideally with reference images and dimensions), a quotation from at least three fabricators, a signed contract with payment terms (typically 50% advance, 50% on delivery), and a lead time of 8–14 weeks minimum. Bespoke items are where most first-time operators underestimate both cost and time.
Lead Time
The time between placing an order and receiving the finished goods. Lead time includes manufacturing time, finishing, quality check, packaging, and delivery. For bespoke fabrications (rolling ladders, chandeliers, Pen Wall): 10–14 weeks. For imported goods (specialty stationery from Europe, or the reading lamps if Indian wholesale can't meet quantity and a China order becomes necessary): 6–10 weeks. For off-the-shelf Indian furniture (bulk order from Kirti Nagar): 4–8 weeks. For technology (assembled cyber café stations from Nehru Place): 2–4 weeks. The procurement timeline in Section 07 is built entirely around respecting these lead times — beginning orders too late is the single most common reason a new campus opens with empty shelves or missing fixtures.
Fit-Out
The process of finishing and furnishing the interior of a building after the structural construction is complete — installing flooring, false ceilings, electrical points, plumbing, joinery, furniture, lighting, and signage. In commercial construction, there are three shell conditions: bare shell (just walls, floor slab, and roof — nothing else), warm shell (electrical and plumbing roughed in, basic flooring), and full fit-out (the finished, operational space). SCRIPTORIUM's fit-out is the work that happens between the architect handing over the warm shell and the campus opening day. The rolling ladders, Pen Wall, stationery shelving, and library furniture are all fit-out items — they cannot be installed until the fit-out is substantially complete.
BOQBill of Quantities
A detailed itemised list of every material, component, and unit of labour required for a construction or fit-out project, with quantities and unit rates, produced by the architect or quantity surveyor. The BOQ is what contractors price against when they submit a tender — it ensures all contractors are quoting on exactly the same scope of work. Document 03 (Cost Estimation) is a pre-design estimate; the BOQ, produced after the architect completes the design, will replace it with precise figures. "Tighten after architect BOQ" in Document 03's header means: the cost estimates in this planning package are accurate to ±20% now, but will become accurate to ±5–10% once the architect produces the BOQ.
MEPMechanical, Electrical & Plumbing
The three building services disciplines that make a building functional beyond its structure: Mechanical (HVAC — heating, ventilation, and air conditioning; fire suppression systems), Electrical (power distribution, lighting circuits, earthing, UPS feeds, data conduits), and Plumbing (water supply, drainage, gas lines for the kitchen). MEP works are designed by specialist MEP engineers and executed during the fit-out phase. In this document, MEP is referenced in the context of: the café kitchen extraction hood (Mechanical), the cyber café's structured cabling conduits (Electrical), and the kitchen's plumbing connections. MEP must be designed before the fit-out begins — adding conduits or drains after walls are finished is extremely expensive.
GSMGrams per Square Metre
The standard measure of paper weight and thickness. A higher GSM means heavier, thicker, more substantial paper. Standard office printer paper is 75–80gsm. Writing paper suitable for fountain pens is 90–100gsm (Rhodia and Clairefontaine use 90gsm as their standard — it shows no bleed-through from even wet fountain pen inks). Watercolour paper starts at 190gsm and goes up to 640gsm for professional sheets. The Paper Sample Wall in SCRIPTORIUM Ink displays a range from lightweight 60gsm tissue through to 300gsm+ card — the GSM label on each sample tells the customer what they are choosing. When sourcing paper for the first floor, always specify GSM explicitly — "good paper" is not a specification.
Fabricator
A workshop or manufacturer that makes physical objects to a custom specification — as distinct from a retailer (who sells existing products) or a contractor (who manages a construction process). In this document, fabricators are the people who build the rolling ladders, cast the chandeliers, construct the Pen Wall panel, and make the staircase treads. Finding a good fabricator requires: seeing their previous work in person (not just photographs), checking that they have experience with the specific material and scale of what you are commissioning, and getting a written quotation that specifies exactly what is and is not included. The cheapest fabricator quotation is rarely the correct one.
Structured Cabling
A planned, standardised network of data and telephone cables installed inside walls, floors, and ceilings during fit-out, running from a central server room or patch panel to every point where a device needs a network connection. For SCRIPTORIUM Studio (the cyber café), structured cabling means Cat6A ethernet cable running from the basement server rack to each of the 20 workstations through concealed conduits in the walls or under raised flooring. Structured cabling must be installed during fit-out — not after. Running cables along skirting boards or over ceiling tiles is a temporary fix that becomes a permanent eyesore. The cost is in Document 04 of this guide; the planning must happen before the fit-out walls are closed.
Technology & Hardware Terms
POS SystemPoint of Sale System
The software and hardware used to process sales transactions — taking orders, charging customers, issuing bills, tracking inventory, and generating daily sales reports. A POS system in a café context replaces a cash register: the barista enters the order on a touchscreen terminal, the kitchen or counter receives a print, and the customer pays by cash, card, or UPI. In a stationery retail context, the POS also tracks which products are selling, flags when stock is running low, and generates end-of-day reports for accounting. The recommendation in this document is Petpooja for the café and Ginesys if SCRIPTORIUM Ink's retail operations are to be integrated into the same system. A POS is not optional — running a café or retail floor without one makes GST compliance, inventory control, and daily reconciliation exponentially harder.
GPUGraphics Processing Unit
The component inside a computer responsible for rendering images, video, and graphical output to the monitor — and increasingly, for accelerating AI, machine learning, and creative software tasks. In SCRIPTORIUM Studio's context, the GPU determines how smoothly the workstations handle video editing, 3D modelling, AI image generation tools, and high-resolution photography work. The specification in this document (RTX 4070 or RTX 4070 Super) is the correct tier for a premium creative workstation in 2026 — capable of handling the full Adobe Creative Suite at full resolution without lag. Going below this tier (RTX 4060) is a false economy: a student doing a film editing session who hits a performance ceiling will not return.
NVMe SSDNon-Volatile Memory Express / Solid State Drive
The type of storage drive used in modern high-performance computers. Unlike a traditional hard disk drive (HDD), which uses spinning magnetic platters, an NVMe SSD has no moving parts — it stores data on flash memory chips and accesses it at speeds 5–10× faster than a standard SATA SSD and 30–50× faster than an HDD. In a cyber café context, NVMe SSD is specified because it dramatically reduces boot time (a station can restart and be ready in under 20 seconds), application load time, and file transfer speed. The Samsung 980 Pro and WD Black SN850X specified in Section 04 are both PCIe 4.0 NVMe drives — the current performance standard for professional workstations.
UPSUninterruptible Power Supply
A battery backup device that keeps equipment running during a power cut and protects against voltage spikes that can damage electronics. A UPS sits between the mains power supply and the equipment it protects — if the mains power fails, the UPS switches to battery power in milliseconds (too fast for equipment to notice) and provides enough time to either keep working or save work and shut down gracefully. For SCRIPTORIUM Studio, a 10kVA online double-conversion UPS is specified — "online" means the equipment always runs from the battery (which is continuously topped up from the mains), eliminating any transition delay during a power cut. In Delhi, where power cuts and voltage fluctuations are a real operational risk, a UPS on the cyber café stations is not optional.
IPS PanelIn-Plane Switching
A type of LCD monitor panel technology that determines colour accuracy and viewing angle. IPS panels offer accurate colour reproduction and consistent image quality when viewed from the side — essential for creative work (photo editing, video editing, graphic design) where colour accuracy matters. The alternative panel types are TN (Twisted Nematic — faster but poor colour and narrow viewing angle) and VA (Vertical Alignment — good contrast but colour shift at angles). All monitors specified in Section 04 are IPS — this is the correct choice for a creative workstation studio. The 1440p resolution (2560×1440) on a 27" monitor gives pixel density high enough for precise design work without the GPU overhead of 4K.
PoEPower over Ethernet
A standard that allows network cables (ethernet) to carry electrical power as well as data — so a single cable can both connect a device to the network and power it, without needing a separate power adapter. In SCRIPTORIUM Studio, a PoE managed switch is specified because it can power Wi-Fi access points and IP cameras throughout the campus through the same Cat6A cabling that carries the network — reducing the number of power outlets and cable runs required. PoE devices (access points, cameras, VoIP phones) are standard in commercial network installations and require no additional electrical work beyond the structured cabling already planned.
NASNetwork Attached Storage
A dedicated file storage device connected to the network, accessible by all computers on that network — essentially a shared hard drive that every workstation can read from and write to. In SCRIPTORIUM Studio, a NAS in the basement server rack provides session storage for users who are working on large files (video projects, design files) that do not fit comfortably on a single workstation's local SSD. It also provides a backup destination: if a user saves work to the NAS during their session, it is still there if the workstation has an issue. The NAS is separate from the internet connection — it is internal to the campus network only.
VESA MountVideo Electronics Standards Association
A standardised pattern of four bolt holes on the back of a monitor that allows it to be attached to a wall bracket, desk arm, or pole mount. Almost all modern monitors (75×75mm or 100×100mm VESA pattern) are VESA compatible. In Section 04, monitor arms are specified for all 20 cyber café stations — a VESA monitor arm clamps to the back of the desk and holds the monitor in a freely adjustable position, eliminating the bulky plastic stand that comes with the monitor and freeing up significant desk space. The Ergotron LX arm specified is the professional standard for commercial workstation environments.
CIRCommitted Information Rate
The minimum guaranteed internet speed a provider will deliver at all times, as opposed to the "headline" speed advertised. Consumer broadband plans often advertise "up to 1Gbps" but the CIR — the speed actually guaranteed under the service agreement — may be a fraction of that, especially during peak hours. For SCRIPTORIUM Studio, a business fibre plan (Jio Enterprise or ACT Business) is specified precisely because business plans carry a higher CIR than residential plans — typically 50–100% CIR versus 10–20% on residential. With 20 stations in simultaneous heavy use, a low CIR means the connection becomes unusable during peak evening hours.
SLAService Level Agreement
A contractual commitment from a service provider on the quality and reliability of their service — typically covering uptime guarantees, maximum response time for fault resolution, and compensation for outages that exceed those thresholds. Business internet plans (as opposed to residential plans) include SLAs: an ISP might guarantee 99.5% uptime and a 4-hour fault resolution window. If the internet goes down in SCRIPTORIUM Studio and the ISP takes 3 days to fix it, a residential plan offers no recourse; a business SLA means either a guaranteed fix time or a credit on the next bill. When comparing ISP quotations for the cyber café, always ask for the SLA document, not just the headline speed and price.
ToSTerms of Service
The legal agreement between a service provider and the user that governs how the service may be used. In the internet connectivity context in Section 04, ToS is referenced because most residential broadband plans explicitly prohibit commercial use — running a cyber café on a residential plan violates the provider's Terms of Service, which gives them grounds to suspend the connection without notice and without compensation. A business internet plan's ToS explicitly permits commercial operation, multiple simultaneous users, and resale of access time to customers. This is not a minor technicality — an ISP can and does enforce this clause.
