00 Why "fines" is the wrong word, and what to use instead

Worth getting right before anything else, because it changes how the rest of this document is written — and because it's the difference between a policy that holds up and one a determined visitor can simply refuse to pay. SCRIPTORIUM is private commercial property. A private business cannot legally impose a "fine" the way a municipal or police authority can — that word describes a state penalty. What SCRIPTORIUM can do, and what gets you the same practical outcome, is three things: set entry conditions a visitor accepts by walking in and using the space, charge for the actual cost of damage they cause, and ask someone to leave if they won't follow the rules.

The one rule that makes this enforceable: under Section 74 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872, a charge for breach — property damage, in this case — is only enforceable if it's a genuine pre-estimate of actual loss, not a number picked to punish. A ₹5,000 "littering fine" invented from nowhere is exactly the kind of clause an unhappy customer could refuse and win. A ₹250 charge tied to "this is what it costs to replace the book you damaged" is straightforwardly enforceable. Every figure in Section 04 below is built the second way on purpose.

This document is not legal advice — it's a working policy draft built on the general principle above. Before this goes on a wall or a website, it's worth ten minutes with an actual lawyer to confirm the exact wording holds up, particularly the liability and security-deposit language in Sections 04 and 06.

01 Campus-wide, every zone

Phones — the rule you specifically asked for

  • Speakerphone calls are not permitted anywhere on campus, in any zone, at any time. No exceptions — not the café, not the Studio. This is the one phone rule that isn't zone-dependent, because there's no zone on this campus where broadcasting a call to everyone nearby is acceptable
  • Reads and Ink: phones on silent; calls taken outside the zone (the Central Atrium Spine bench run, Document 02, is the natural spot) or with headphones for a brief, quiet call
  • Capsule: same as Reads — pods are for focus, not calls
  • Studio and A&M Munchies: normal conversational-volume calls are fine; the speakerphone rule still applies

Noise, more generally

  • Reads and Ink are quiet zones by design (Document 02's acoustic treatment assumes library-level noise, not café-level)
  • Capsule pods are individually enclosed — a normal conversation inside one is fine for the occupant, but playing audio without headphones is not
  • Studio and A&M Munchies run at normal café/workspace volume

Photography

  • Personal photography for social use is welcome everywhere — the whole point of several design decisions in Document 02 is that people will want to photograph this building
  • Professional/commercial shoots (influencer content with lighting equipment, brand shoots, filming) need advance permission from the Campus Director — this protects both other visitors' privacy and the brand control Document 04 already establishes

Food and drink, by zone

  • Reads, Capsule, Ink, Studio: no outside food. Covered drinks (not open cups) are fine at Reads and Studio desks specifically — Ink's merchandise and Capsule's electronics make even covered drinks a bad idea there
  • A&M Munchies: the exception zone — this is where food and drink happen, including bringing café items to a Reads table if you'd rather read with a coffee (covered cup only)

03 No single-use plastic bottles — and what we give you instead

This isn't a bare restriction; it only works if there's a genuinely better option on offer, and SCRIPTORIUM already has the water infrastructure to make that easy. The campus runs on a rainwater-harvesting and pond system specifically because a borewell risked a CGWA refusal in this part of Delhi (Document 01) — a plastic-bottle culture sits oddly next to that.

The rule

  • No single-use plastic water bottles carried openly into Reads, Capsule, Ink, or Studio — visitors arriving with one are asked to decant it or leave it at the entry point, not confiscated or thrown away
  • A&M Munchies may still sell bottled beverages where no reasonable alternative exists (a sealed soft drink, for instance) — this rule targets plain water bottles specifically, where a free alternative exists on-site

The alternative — filtered water stations, exact locations

  • One RO/UV filtered water dispenser at each of the two washroom cores — ground floor and upper floor (Document 02) — the natural stop for anyone already passing that way
  • One at the Central Atrium Spine entry, immediately past the Gothic portal — this is the one every first-time visitor sees, and it's the natural hand-off point for "leave your bottle here" if someone arrives carrying one
  • One at the Ink ground-floor entrance — Ink is the one zone spanning three floors on its own, so a visitor heading straight up to floor two or three from the street shouldn't have to detour to a washroom core first
  • Four dispensers total. Studio and A&M Munchies aren't separately served — Studio users are already steps from the spine dispenser, and A&M Munchies sells drinks directly
  • A&M Munchies or Ink could sell a branded steel/glass SCRIPTORIUM bottle as a small retail line — turns a restriction into a souvenir and a minor revenue line, worth a note for Document 08's product mix if it's not already there

Security deposits, where they make sense

  • Capsule booking: a modest refundable deposit (or a card hold) at booking, released after the pod is checked — standard practice for a space this well-fitted-out, and it means most damage conversations never need to happen at all
  • Book borrowing (if/when Reads adds a lending option beyond reference use): tied to a membership record rather than cash, so a lost book is recoverable without an awkward front-desk transaction

Where this stops being a cost-recovery conversation

  • Deliberate vandalism, theft, or assault are criminal matters under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (which replaced the IPC) — those go to the police, not a house-rules invoice
  • The Campus Director makes that call, not front-line staff — see Section 06

05 Litter and waste

Delhi's own municipal solid-waste bylaws set public-space littering penalties from ₹200 to ₹10,000 depending on severity and location. SCRIPTORIUM, as private property, enforces this through its own house rules rather than MCD's public-space mechanism — but for anyone who litters deliberately or after already being asked once, the charge below sits at the top of that same government range, not below it. The reasoning: a first-time miss deserves a word, not a bill, but someone who does it again despite being told isn't having an accident anymore, and the response should say so. The pond specifically matters here too: Document 01 already flags that debris and stagnant organic waste risk mosquito-breeding complaints under MCD's anti-malaria bylaws, so litter near the pond isn't just untidy, it's a genuine compliance issue Document 10's landscape plan was designed around.

1
First instance — a word, not a charge
Staff point out the nearest bin (the table above shows exactly where) and ask the visitor to use it. This resolves the overwhelming majority of littering on the spot, and nobody is charged anything for a genuine first-time miss.
2
Deliberate or repeated — the top of the MCD scale
₹10,000 — the ceiling of Delhi's own municipal littering-penalty range, not a number invented for deterrence. This is a deliberate change from an earlier, lighter draft: a modest cleaning-cost charge works for genuine accidents, but does nothing against someone who litters again after already being asked once. Pegging the charge to the maximum the state itself considers proportionate for this exact offence keeps it defensible under the Section 74 standard — it isn't an arbitrary number, it's the ceiling Delhi's own bylaws already treat as reasonable for littering specifically. Reserved for the second instance onward, or any littering that's plainly deliberate (thrown rather than dropped, done in view of a "please use the bin" sign) rather than every accidental miss.
3
Near the pond specifically — the ₹10,000 charge plus removal
Given the mosquito-breeding compliance risk, littering in the pond/backyard zone carries the same ₹10,000 charge as Step 2 and an immediate request to leave for the day — this is the one place on campus where the environmental and regulatory stakes go beyond a cleanliness issue.

06 Who enforces this, and how it escalates

The escalation ladder

  • Verbal request from any staff member on the floor
  • A specific ask from the zone's senior role (Head Librarian, Ink Floor Manager, Studio Manager, Head Barista) if the first ask doesn't land
  • Security involvement (Document 11) for anything that doesn't resolve at the zone level
  • Campus Director decides on cost-recovery charges, deposit forfeiture, or asking someone to leave for the day
  • Police, only for theft, vandalism, or assault — genuinely rare, and the Campus Director's call, not a front-line staff decision

What front-line staff should never do

  • Physically restrain or confront a visitor — de-escalate and involve Security instead
  • Invent a charge on the spot that isn't in Section 04's table — anything not listed gets an actual quotation, not a guessed number
  • Make it personal — the rule is being enforced, not the individual being punished; Document 06's brand-voice standard applies here too

07 What this document is deliberately not restricting

Worth stating outright, because a rules document can accidentally read as more restrictive than intended, and that's the opposite of what a "place where knowledge is held, worked with, and made" (Document 04) should feel like.

Explicitly fine

  • Reading aloud softly to a child in Reads
  • A quiet conversation between two people studying together
  • Sitting for hours over one coffee at A&M Munchies outside peak hours
  • Bringing a personal laptop or tablet anywhere on campus

The spirit of the whole document

  • Every rule above exists to protect the experience for everyone else in the room, not to police behaviour for its own sake — if a staff member is ever unsure whether something crosses a line, "does this affect anyone else's experience" is the actual test

08 Making it enforceable — how visitors actually see this

A house rule only holds up if a visitor had reasonable notice of it before the situation arose — this is a real principle, not just good practice, and it's the other half of Section 00's enforceability point. That means these rules need to live somewhere a visitor plausibly saw them: entry signage, the website, and a QR code at each zone threshold.

Draft entry signage copy — short enough to actually read

Welcome to SCRIPTORIUM

  1. Silence in Reads, Ink & Capsule — calls outside, please
  2. No speakerphone, anywhere
  3. Water refill stations throughout — no need for a bottle
  4. You break it, you replace it — ask any of us if that's ever unclear
  5. Full house rules: scriptorium.in/visit — or the QR code beside this sign

Typography and layout for this signage should follow Document 04's Gill Sans wayfinding standard, not the Garamond display face — this is operational text, not brand display.
