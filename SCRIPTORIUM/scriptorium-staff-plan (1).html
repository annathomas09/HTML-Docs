Founder: Anna Thomas Total headcount: 19–22 people Total monthly payroll: ₹4.5–6.1 lakh Salary basis: Delhi NCR market rates, June 2026 Emphasis: Quality over convenience, always

00 The one thing that determines whether SCRIPTORIUM works

The architecture can be perfect, the location can be ideal, the brand can be exactly right — and the campus can still fail if the wrong people are running it. SCRIPTORIUM's quality promise is communicated entirely through the people inside it: through the librarian who knows where every book is, through the barista who makes the coffee correctly every single time, through the stationery associate who knows which ink works in which nib. You cannot automate any of this. You cannot fake it with good signage. The staff IS the brand, in every moment of every operating day.

The SCRIPTORIUM hiring principle: Hire for disposition first, skills second, experience third. Skills can be taught in two weeks. A genuine love for what the role requires cannot be trained into someone who doesn't have it. The head librarian who actually reads is worth more than five library science graduates who don't. The barista who is obsessed with extraction is worth more than ten people who have memorised the menu. Every hiring decision in this document follows this principle.

What SCRIPTORIUM never compromises on: No candidate who cannot speak about the campus with the warmth and precision described in the brand voice section (Section 07 of the Brand Identity document) should be hired for any customer-facing role, regardless of their experience, qualifications, or how urgently the position needs to be filled. An empty desk serves visitors better than the wrong person behind it.

There are no middle management layers between zone leads and the Campus Director — SCRIPTORIUM is not large enough to justify them, and they would slow the decision-making that keeps a premium multi-zone campus running smoothly.

02 Three hiring tiers — not all roles are equal

Different roles have different consequences if filled poorly. A tier-1 hire that goes wrong can close the campus; a tier-3 hire that goes wrong is an inconvenience. Understanding which tier each role falls in tells you how much time, care, and budget to invest in the selection process.

TierWhat it meansRolesHiring process
Tier 1 · Critical Get this wrong and the whole campus suffers permanently. These roles define the visitor's experience and the campus's reputation. Campus Director · Head Librarian · Head Barista/A&M Manager Minimum 4-stage process. No shortcuts. Take months if needed.
Tier 2 · High Impact Important and visible roles where the wrong person causes real damage, but the damage is zone-specific rather than campus-wide. Ink Floor Manager · Studio Manager · Capsule Host 3-stage process. Trial shift required. References checked.
Tier 3 · Important Necessary and must be good, but trainable within 2–4 weeks. Errors are correctable before they affect visitor experience. Library Assistants · Floor Associates · Café Staff · Maintenance · Security 2-stage process. Strong trial period. TAU internship pipeline preferred.

03 Management layer

Campus Director
1 person · Tier 1
Monthly salary₹65,000 – ₹85,000
Tier 1 — Critical hire

This person is the campus. Every visitor experience, every zone's operational consistency, every staff relationship, and every piece of feedback that reaches you as the founder goes through or is shaped by this person. The Campus Director does not manage one zone — they hold all five zones together while you are absent, and they make real-time decisions that reflect the SCRIPTORIUM standard without waiting to ask you. This is the hardest hire in the document and should never be rushed.

What you are looking for

  • Has run a multi-zone hospitality, cultural, or premium retail operation before — not a single-zone manager. They must have navigated the complexity of different teams with different rhythms under one roof.
  • Thinks about quality the way you do — if their definition of a good day is "no complaints," they are the wrong person. The right person's definition of a good day is "the library was full and the pods were quiet."
  • Is comfortable being the founder's representative — they will be Anna's voice in every interaction that Anna is not present for. They must understand the brand voice, the vision, and the standards well enough to embody them.
  • Manages downward with warmth and upward with honesty — zone staff should want to work for them; the founder should be able to trust their reports completely.
  • Is genuinely curious about knowledge and culture — someone who has no relationship with books, stationery, or study culture cannot represent SCRIPTORIUM convincingly.

Dealbreakers — reject immediately if

  • Has only managed a single-format operation (only cafés, only retail) — a SCRIPTORIUM Director must think across zones simultaneously
  • Defines quality as "no complaints" — the absence of complaints is not quality; it is the floor, not the ceiling
  • Cannot name three books they have read recently — not because reading is required for the job, but because someone who doesn't read in their own life will never genuinely believe in what SCRIPTORIUM Reads is for
  • Has no experience managing people who are specialists — the Head Librarian and Head Barista are both subject-matter experts; managing them requires a different skill than managing general retail staff

Where to find them

  • Premium cultural venues in Delhi — IHC, India Habitat Centre, NGMA, LHI
  • Multi-concept hospitality groups — someone who has managed an F&B + retail combination in a premium context
  • Senior managers from café chains with strong culture (Blue Tokai, Third Wave, Perch)
  • Not from placement agencies — post the role publicly and screen personally

04 SCRIPTORIUM Reads + Capsule

Head Librarian
1 person · Tier 1
Monthly salary₹35,000 – ₹48,000
Tier 1 — Critical hire

The Head Librarian is the soul of SCRIPTORIUM Reads. Their relationship with the collection — knowing what is in it, where it is, who it is for, what is missing, and what should never be there — determines whether the library feels like a curated intellectual sanctuary or a room full of shelved paper. The government librarian average in Delhi runs ₹25,000–₹31,000/month; SCRIPTORIUM pays above this to attract a private-sector candidate who has chosen books over a government job because they love the work, not the security.

What you are looking for

  • Reads personally and widely — this is non-negotiable. Ask in the interview: what have you read this month? What would you add to a collection serving UPSC aspirants, DU students, and casual readers equally? Their answers tell you everything.
  • BLISc or MLISc degree preferred — but not a dealbreaker if the candidate has deep independent reading culture and some catalogue/system experience
  • Experience with Dewey Decimal or similar classification — the open shelving system must be intuitive enough that a visitor can navigate it without asking
  • Has opinions about curation — should be able to argue for and against including specific books. A librarian with no preferences is not a curator, they are a shelf-stocker.
  • Warm and legible in person — visitors will ask them for recommendations; they must be approachable without being performatively helpful

Dealbreakers

  • Does not read fiction and non-fiction both — a librarian who only reads one genre cannot curate a general collection
  • Is uncomfortable recommending books unprompted — passive librarians who wait to be asked produce passive libraries
  • Comes from a school or government library only — the SCRIPTORIUM Reads model is closer to a curated bookshop than a school library; institutional library mindset does not transfer directly

Also manages

  • The rented book system for SCRIPTORIUM Capsule — tracking which books are in which pods, condition on return, replenishment rotation
  • Monthly "new arrivals" selection — must have a small acquisition budget to refresh the collection and a process for deciding what leaves
  • Library events — evening talks, author visits, reading circles, in coordination with Ink Floor Manager for joint programming

Where to find them

  • Delhi University library science faculty — MLISc graduates looking for a premium private role rather than a government posting; DU's DLIS department at North Campus is the primary source
  • India Habitat Centre and LHI (Living History India) — both run curated members' libraries in South Delhi; their active staff or volunteers are precisely the reading-culture profile this role demands
  • Delhi's literary event circuit — regular attendees and volunteers at Apeejay Lit Fest, Delhi Literature Festival, and Kitab events often have the depth of personal reading this role requires
  • Nonprofit libraries in Delhi (Sakshat, Kutumb Foundation, Delhi Public Library branches) — someone who chose a mission-driven reading institution over a government posting signals the right disposition
  • Do not post on Naukri or LinkedIn alone — post in literary communities: the DU Reading Club, Delhi Poetry Slam, and local book clubs whose members will circulate it to exactly the right people

05 SCRIPTORIUM Ink — 3 Themed Floors

Ink Floor Manager & Buyer
1 person · Tier 2
Monthly salary₹32,000 – ₹45,000
Tier 2 — High impact

The Itoya model only works if the person running the stationery tower is as invested in the products as the visitors are. This is not a generic retail floor manager role — it is a specialist buyer and curator who happens to also manage a team. They decide what goes on the shelves, how it is displayed, which workshops are programmed, and which vendor relationships to build. The market rate for a retail store manager in Delhi is ₹30,000–₹50,000/month depending on experience; SCRIPTORIUM targets the upper range to get someone with genuine stationery enthusiasm and not just retail operations experience.

What you are looking for

  • Is personally passionate about stationery — ask: what pen are you currently using? What paper do you prefer? A candidate who cannot answer these questions is not the right person for this role.
  • Has retail buying or merchandising experience — understands vendor relationships, margin management, inventory rotation, and seasonal buying cycles
  • Knows the Indian stationery market specifically — Camlin, Natraj, Faber-Castell India, William Penn — and ideally has contacts in the niche import market (Japanese stationery, European inks)
  • Can programme workshops — origami, calligraphy, watercolour — either as the instructor themselves or by sourcing and briefing external instructors
  • Has managed a small team — three floor associates report to this person

Dealbreakers

  • Generic retail background without stationery or art supply specialisation — someone who has managed a clothing store or electronics retail brings the wrong instincts to product display and buying
  • No interest in the Itoya model specifically — if they are not excited by the idea of three themed floors with a pen wall and a try-table when you describe it in the interview, they will not maintain the standard
  • Focused only on sales targets — the Ink floors must feel like a destination, not a shop pushing units. Revenue matters, but it follows from curation, not from sales pressure.

Where to find them

  • William Penn store managers or buyers (Delhi has a William Penn outlet in Connaught Place — they may have staff looking for growth)
  • Art supply store managers from Golcha Market or Chandni Chowk's stationery wholesale district
  • Active fountain pen community members — the Indian fountain pen online community (r/IndiaPens, FPN India) is small but passionate and sometimes produces exactly this profile

08 Support — Maintenance & Security

Maintenance & Housekeeping
2 people · Tier 3
Monthly salary₹12,000 – ₹15,000

SCRIPTORIUM is a premium space — a dirty floor in the library, an unwatered plant in the café, or a finger-marked pod panel undermines everything the brand communicates. Maintenance staff must understand that their work is as brand-critical as anyone else's. Two people, staggered shifts: one opening (arrives 1 hour before visitors, cleans and sets up the campus), one closing (cleans after last visitor, prepares for next day). Both are also responsible for the backyard pond and landscape watering rota.

What you are looking for

  • Methodical and thorough — a checklist mentality, not "I cleaned what looked dirty"
  • Comfortable with delicate materials — Kadappa stone floors, teak shelving, and terracotta jaali screens all require specific cleaning methods; training on these is provided
  • Minor repair capability — changing a light bulb, fixing a loose shelf bracket, replacing a pod indicator LED; escalates structural issues to Campus Director
  • Reliable over everything else — consistent attendance matters more than any other quality in this role

Security (2 people, rotating shifts) — direct hire, not agency

  • SCRIPTORIUM hires and employs security staff directly — a deliberate change from the original agency-sourced plan. This is legally straightforward: the Private Security Agencies (Regulation) Act, 2005 (PSARA) licenses businesses that are in the business of providing security guards to others as a service — it doesn't require an end-user company to hold a licence just to employ its own guards, the same way SCRIPTORIUM doesn't need any special licence to employ its own librarians or baristas. No PSARA licence is needed for SCRIPTORIUM itself under this model
  • ₹16,000–₹20,000/month · genuinely higher take-home than the ₹13,000–16,000 an agency would have billed — because that agency figure was never the guard's full salary in the first place. A meaningful share of any agency's billed rate is the agency's own margin (recruitment, overhead, profit), not wages passed to the guard. Direct hire redirects that margin straight into pay, which is the practical mechanism behind "pay them better than agencies," not just a nice sentiment
  • Primary function at SCRIPTORIUM: entry management and ambience protection, not adversarial security. The security person at the door is the first physical impression of the campus — they must be presentable, calm, and polite. Their role in the visitor-rules escalation ladder (Document 12) is the second-to-last step, after zone staff and before the Campus Director — most situations should never reach them at all
  • Also responsible for the nightly building lock-up sequence and first-line CCTV monitoring — see Document 13 for exactly where the campus's CCTV obligation comes from (it's the A&M Munchies Eating House Licence, not the Studio's IT Act status) and the full camera placement plan
  • The full recruitment, physical-assessment, and training protocol — including what SCRIPTORIUM now takes on that a PSARA-licensed agency used to handle automatically — is Document 13's Section 03, not repeated here

Where to find both roles

  • IHM Pusa housekeeping module graduates — hotel management students who specialised in housekeeping operations arrive trained in material-specific cleaning, which is exactly what Kadappa stone floors and teak shelving require
  • The local Said-ul-Ajaib and surrounding village community — for maintenance staff especially, proximity to the campus is the single most important practical factor; a reliable person who lives ten minutes away is worth more than a trained person who lives forty-five minutes away and arrives late on difficult mornings
  • Preference for female maintenance staff on the morning shift — they finish before peak visitor hours, are statistically more methodical in checklist adherence, and align with the campus's financial-empowerment ethos
  • For security specifically: sourcing candidates directly means casting a wider net than an agency's existing roster — ex-servicemen associations (Army, Navy, Air Force, Delhi Police retirees) are the strongest single channel, per Document 13's Section 03, and PSARA's own guidance gives the same preference to candidates with armed-forces or police background

What direct hire does and doesn't change administratively: Section 09a below already has an HR consultant handling EPFO/ESIC registration and monthly payroll filing for every SCRIPTORIUM employee. Adding 2 security staff to that same direct-payroll structure is not a new category of administrative work — it's 2 more names on a system that already exists for the other 17 roles. What direct hire does add that an agency was quietly absorbing: SCRIPTORIUM now owns police verification, ongoing training, and shift-replacement coverage itself rather than outsourcing them. Document 13 covers all three.

09a Who hires, who trains — do you need an HR person?

At 19 staff, SCRIPTORIUM is not large enough to justify a full-time HR person. A dedicated HR manager typically becomes cost-effective at 50+ employees or 3+ locations — neither of which applies at launch. The hiring and people-management function is distributed across three layers:

Anna (founder) — personally makes every Tier 1 decision. Campus Director, Head Librarian, Head Barista. These three hires shape everything else. They are never delegated, never rushed, and never made on the basis of a candidate's availability rather than their quality. Anna also conducts the final probation review for every Tier 1 hire at Day 90.

Campus Director — manages all Tier 2 and Tier 3 hiring after Day 1, with Anna's approval on all offers. Also owns: daily HR operations (rosters, leave tracking, performance conversations, grievance resolution), staff culture enforcement, and the 30/60-day probation check-ins for all junior staff. This is why the Campus Director hire matters so disproportionately — they are effectively the HR department for the first three years.

Part-time HR consultant (retainer) — ₹8,000–₹12,000/month. Not a full employee. Handles: employment contracts and offer letters, EPFO/ESIC registration and monthly filing, POSH Act compliance documentation (the Internal Complaints Committee must be formally constituted from the first female hire), payroll structuring, and any labour law query. This is the minimum statutory compliance infrastructure and must be in place before the first salary is paid — not retroactively.

09b When to start hiring — the timeline

The most common mistake in new campus openings is hiring too late — the right people do not materialise in two weeks, and opening with undertrained or mismatched staff produces an experience that no amount of architectural quality can compensate for. Use T to mean the planned opening day and count backwards.

T−18 mo
Construction Begins
Begin long-listing the Campus Director
Start mapping the talent landscape — attend Delhi hospitality and cultural institution events, note who is running multi-zone premium operations well. Do not post the role yet. You are building a network of candidates to approach when the time is right. The Campus Director is too important to find through a posted job ad alone; they are found through deliberate outreach.
T−12 mo
Mid-Construction
Campus Director hired — begins as a paid consultant
The Campus Director joins on a consulting retainer (₹40,000–₹55,000/month) initially. They attend final construction review meetings, begin vendor identification, and start building operational systems (booking software, inventory management, staff rosters). 09d Working hours, leave, holidays & festival bonuses

SCRIPTORIUM operates 9am–10pm, seven days a week. Staff work in two shifts under the Delhi Shops and Establishments Act, which sets the legal maximum at 9 working hours per day (including a mandatory 1-hour break) and 48 hours per week, with overtime paid at 1.5× the regular rate for any hours beyond this. Every staff member gets one fixed weekly day off, rotated so the campus remains fully staffed every day.

Two daily shifts

  • Opening shift: 8:00am – 5:00pm (8 hours work + 1-hour break). Arrives before visitors, opens the campus, handles the morning peak.
  • Closing shift: 1:00pm – 10:00pm (8 hours work + 1-hour break). Handles afternoon and evening peak, closes the campus.
  • Overlap from 1pm–5pm ensures full coverage during the busiest hours of the day.
  • Weekly roster is set by the Campus Director two weeks in advance, published in writing, and not changed without 48 hours' notice except in genuine emergencies.
  • No staff member works more than 6 consecutive days without a day off.

Annual leave entitlement

  • Earned Leave: 18 days/year — 3 more than the Delhi S&E Act minimum of 15; the extra 3 days are a SCRIPTORIUM benefit, not a statutory obligation
  • Casual Leave: 8 days/year (per Delhi S&E Act)
  • Sick Leave: 12 days/year; medical certificate required after 3 consecutive sick days
  • National & Restricted Holidays: 14 per year (per Central Government notification — includes Republic Day, Independence Day, Gandhi Jayanti, and 11 restricted holidays)
  • Total: 52 days of leave per year. A staff member at SCRIPTORIUM is off approximately 1 day in every 7 on average.

Female employee provisions

  • Maternity leave: 26 weeks paid — mandatory under the Maternity Benefit Act 2017 for females who have worked 80 days in the preceding 12 months. This applies to the first two children. SCRIPTORIUM pays full salary throughout.
  • POSH Act compliance — an Internal Complaints Committee (ICC) must be constituted from the first female hire. The ICC requires a minimum of 3 members: a presiding officer (senior female employee), one other employee, and one external member from an NGO or legal background. The HR consultant sets this up. Non-compliance attracts penalties from Day 1 of the first female hire.
  • No mandatory late-evening shift for female staff without written consent — the closing shift ends at 10pm, which is within manageable commute hours but requires prior agreement in the employment contract for each individual
  • Evening transport support: ₹600/month for any female staff member whose closing shift ends past 8pm — covers auto or app-cab cost from the campus to the nearest metro station or home. A small amount with a significant signal.
  • No night shift — SCRIPTORIUM closes at 10pm; the latest any staff member is on-site is 10:30pm for close-down. This is not a night-shift environment.
  • Menstrual leave: 2 days per month, paid, self-certified. Available to all female employees from Day 1 — not after probation, not after a qualifying period. No medical certificate required, ever. No justification required, ever. Tracked by the Campus Director as a confidential leave code with no disclosure obligation. Cannot be accumulated across months or converted to cash. This is not sick leave — it is a separate entitlement that exists because menstrual pain is real, varies significantly between individuals, and should never require a woman to work through debilitating pain or spend a sick-leave day on something that is not an illness.
  • Period products in staff restrooms at no cost — pads, tampons, and a disposal unit, restocked monthly. A negligible budget item (approximately ₹200–300/month for the full staff) with a significant daily signal about what kind of workplace SCRIPTORIUM is.
  • If the campus has a staff rest area, female staff experiencing severe menstrual pain may use it to lie down during a break without being required to clock out or use leave for that period — this is an operational arrangement managed by the Campus Director with discretion and without paperwork.

09 The SCRIPTORIUM hiring process

Every tier-1 role follows all five stages. Tier-2 roles follow stages 1–4. Tier-3 roles follow stages 1, 2, and 5. No stage is optional for its tier. The most common mistake in small premium businesses is skipping Stage 3 (the practical test) for convenience — this is where the most important information about a candidate is revealed, and skipping it accounts for the majority of bad hires.

01
The written brief — before the interview
Every candidate for any role receives a one-page brief about SCRIPTORIUM before they are interviewed — the name, the concept, the five zones, the founding philosophy. They are asked to respond in writing (not more than 300 words): "What about SCRIPTORIUM interests you, and what question does reading this brief make you want to ask?" This single response tells you whether the candidate has engaged with the brand seriously. A generic response ("I am excited to work in a dynamic environment") is an immediate disqualification. You are looking for a response that shows they actually thought about the specific nature of the campus.
02
The knowledge interview — 45 minutes
A structured conversation covering: (a) relevant experience, (b) the specific knowledge test for the role (for the Head Librarian: name three books you would recommend to a UPSC aspirant who has never read history voluntarily; for the Head Barista: walk me through how you would adjust your extraction if your espresso started tasting sour mid-service; for the Ink Floor Manager: which product would you put on the try-table to introduce a customer to fountain pens for the first time and why), and (c) a culture alignment question about how they define quality in their current role. These are not gotcha questions — they are tests of genuine engagement with the craft. No script answers, no "I am a quick learner" deflections accepted.
03
The practical test — on-site, paid half-day
Every shortlisted candidate works a paid half-day (₹500–800 depending on role) in their zone. The Head Librarian candidate is given a list of 30 books and asked to arrange them into a section of shelving with signage. The Head Barista candidate makes coffee on your machine for a panel of three people and is observed adjusting to feedback. The Studio Manager candidate is given a machine with a deliberately introduced fault and asked to diagnose it. The Ink Floor Manager candidate is given 20 products and asked to set up the Ground Floor try-table for a Saturday morning. This stage is non-negotiable. What a candidate does in half a day tells you far more than any interview answer.
04
References — minimum two, verified by phone
For tier-1 and tier-2 hires only. References must be from direct managers or supervisors, not colleagues. The three questions asked of every reference: (1) Would you hire them again? Why or why not? (2) What were they like on a bad day — when something went wrong? (3) What would they need to improve to reach the next level? Reference letters are not accepted — phone calls only, where tone and hesitation are as informative as the words. A reference who gives only positive answers to all three questions is a red flag, not a green one; real endorsements include honest qualifications.
05
Probationary period — 90 days with structured review
Every new hire, regardless of tier, enters a 90-day probationary period with written performance checkpoints at Day 30 and Day 60. The Day 30 review covers: has the person learned the physical systems (the library catalogue, the booking software, the machine setup)? The Day 60 review covers: are they making quality decisions independently, or do they still defer everything upward? The Day 90 confirmation review is the Campus Director's formal recommendation to the founder. A failed probation should be acted on immediately — the most expensive mistake is keeping the wrong person out of discomfort with having the conversation.

12 Staff culture — how SCRIPTORIUM people carry themselves

The brand voice section in the identity document applies to every member of staff, not just management. Every person on the floor is a spokesperson for SCRIPTORIUM whether or not they have been told so. These are the behaviours that reinforce the brand and the ones that erode it. What they wear while doing it is covered separately — see Document 11 (Staff Uniform & Dress Code) for the full uniform system by role, plus grooming, religious/cultural accommodation, and weather adaptation standards.

The SCRIPTORIUM standard

  • Speak in complete sentences. Never "Yeah, that's on the second floor." Always "The Paper & Letter floor is directly above us — the staircase is at the end of this passage."
  • Know the answer before you give it. If you do not know, say so and find out — do not guess and commit to a wrong answer.
  • The campus is clean because you maintain it, not because housekeeping will get to it eventually. If you see something out of place, address it.
  • Make eye contact when a visitor enters your zone. Acknowledge without being intrusive. One unhurried "Good morning" is worth more than three energetic "Welcome!"s.
  • Know enough about every zone to direct visitors accurately. A library assistant should know how the stationery floors are organised; a café staff member should know which pods are bookable.

What erodes the brand immediately

  • Referring to the campus as "the place" or "the store." It is always SCRIPTORIUM, by name.
  • Using a mobile phone on the floor during operating hours. No exceptions for any role.
  • Discussing internal operations, staff conflicts, or business matters within earshot of visitors.
  • Pointing rather than walking a visitor to where they need to go, when it takes less than 60 seconds to walk them there.
  • Telling a visitor something is not available without offering an alternative. "We don't have X" is never the complete sentence. "We don't have X, but Y might work for what you need — let me show you" is.
