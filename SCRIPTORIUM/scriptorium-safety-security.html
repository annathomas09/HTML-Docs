01 Fire safety — the NOC, and what actually goes where

Document 01 already established that Fire NOC is required for all occupied commercial space on this site. Worth stating the specific rule that makes this unambiguous rather than just asserted: Delhi Fire Service Rules, 2010, Rule 27 requires NOC for institutional and mercantile buildings taller than 9 metres or with three or more storeys. SCRIPTORIUM clears that threshold by a wide margin on two counts alone — the Reads tower is 22–26m, and Ink is three floors on its own — so this was never a borderline case.

The two-stage process

  • Stage 1 — Provisional NOC: filed with the architect's building plans, before construction. Delhi Fire Service reviews the fire-fighting scheme drawings (extinguisher locations, alarm/detection layout, exit routes, staircases) and issues design-stage recommendations
  • Stage 2 — Final Fire Safety Certificate (FSC): issued under Rule 35, after all recommended systems are installed and verified on-site — this is the certificate that actually permits occupancy
  • Validity: 3 years for non-residential buildings from date of issue, then renewal

What governs the technical spec

  • IS 2190 (Bureau of Indian Standards) governs extinguisher selection, placement, mounting height, and signage — matched to fire risk type per zone
  • National Building Code 2016, Part 4 sets the broader technical standard for detection, fire-fighting systems, evacuation routes, and compartmentation that the Fire NOC drawings must satisfy
  • Any loss from a non-functional fire safety system is the owner/occupier's liability, including legal proceedings — Delhi Fire Service is explicit that this responsibility doesn't transfer to whoever sold or installed the equipment

02 CCTV — the corrected legal basis, and where cameras actually go

Correction from earlier drafts: Document 03 and Document 06 both said CCTV was "required under the IT Act" or "cyber café regulations." That's not accurate, and it's worth being precise about why, since the actual rule that applies is stronger and more specific than the one that got cited. The IT Act's Cyber Cafe Rules, 2011 require identity verification and a log register retained for a minimum of one year — a text record of who used a computer and when, not video surveillance. Studio still needs that log register; it just isn't where the CCTV requirement comes from.

The real source: the Delhi Police Eating House Licence. A&M Munchies serves food, which makes an Eating House Licence mandatory under the Delhi Police Act — and functional CCTV with a minimum 30-day recording backup is an explicit, checked condition of that licence, verified by a police site inspection alongside fire exits and hygiene standards. Because A&M Munchies sits inside the same building as every other zone, the practical outcome is the same one this campus already planned for — campus-wide CCTV — but the licensing trigger and the retention period (30 days, not one year) are different from what was written before.

Monitoring, retention, and one more law worth knowing about

Operational

  • Live monitoring is one of Security's stated responsibilities (Document 06, Section 08; full protocol in Section 03 below)
  • 30-day rolling retention minimum, matching the Eating House Licence condition — footage older than 30 days can be safely overwritten unless it's part of an active incident review
  • Access to stored footage restricted to the Campus Director and on-duty Security — not general staff

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023

  • CCTV footage and the Studio log register both count as personal data under India's 2023 data protection law — SCRIPTORIUM is the "Data Fiduciary" for this data, meaning purpose limitation, reasonable security safeguards, and breach notification obligations all apply
  • Practically: don't use footage for anything beyond safety/security and the specific licensing purpose it was installed for, and don't hand it to a third party without a genuine legal basis (police request, for instance) — this is a newer, still-developing area of Indian law, worth a compliance-lawyer conversation once the system is actually being installed rather than treated as fully settled by this document

03 Security staffing — the full direct-hire protocol

This is the section Document 06 and Document 11 both point to. Moving from agency to direct hire is legally straightforward — but it does move a few things that an agency was quietly handling onto SCRIPTORIUM's own plate, and this section is what replaces them.

Why this doesn't require SCRIPTORIUM to hold a PSARA licence: the Private Security Agencies (Regulation) Act, 2005 licenses businesses in the business of providing private security guards to others as a service. SCRIPTORIUM employing its own two guards, the same way it employs its own librarians and baristas, isn't "carrying on the business of a private security agency" under the Act — it's just an employer with a security role on its own payroll. The licensing requirement, the 160-hour mandatory training curriculum, and the agency-level compliance apparatus all attach to agencies, not to end-user employers hiring directly.

Recruitment channels

Where to look

  • Ex-servicemen associations — Army, Navy, Air Force, and Delhi Police retiree networks are the single strongest channel; PSARA's own statutory guidance gives hiring preference to candidates with this background, for good reason — they arrive already disciplined, trained in basic protocol, and comfortable with a structured role
  • Delhi Police's own retired-personnel welfare associations often maintain informal placement networks specifically for this kind of civilian security role
  • Direct referral from other premium South Delhi commercial properties (malls, hotels, embassies) — ask their facilities/security manager who they'd recommend from their own rejected-but-solid candidate pool

What to screen for at the resume stage

  • Prior armed-forces, police, or home-guard service (strong positive signal, not a hard requirement)
  • Prior civilian security experience specifically in a hospitality or retail setting, not only industrial/warehouse security — the role is described as ambience protection, and that's a different skill than warehouse perimeter duty
  • Basic spoken English or Hindi-English comfort — this is a customer-facing role at a premium campus, and the person at the door needs to handle a visitor's question, not just stand at it

Police verification

The single most important thing an agency was doing that SCRIPTORIUM must now do itself. This is a standard, well-established process, not a novel one:

1
Character/antecedent verification at the local police station
Standard practice for any security-adjacent hire in Delhi, done through the jurisdictional police station covering the candidate's residential address — the same character-verification process landlords and many employers already use, just applied here with the seriousness the role deserves.
2
Reference checks with the two most recent employers
Phone calls, not letters — Document 06's own reference-check standard ("what were they like on a bad day") applies just as much here as to a Tier 1 hire, arguably more, given the trust the role carries.
3
Document verification
Aadhaar, address proof, and — if applicable — any existing PSARA-issued private security guard certification from prior agency employment, which is a genuine positive signal even though SCRIPTORIUM itself doesn't need to be PSARA-licensed to hire the person holding it.

Physical assessment — elevated, still proportionate, still not a mismatch for the job

The role is still fundamentally "entry management and ambience protection" (Document 06) — a library, café, and stationery campus, not a compound. But "ambience protection" now explicitly includes being someone's actual first line against a forced-entry or crowd situation until police arrive, given that arrival may be slower here than elsewhere. The assessment below reflects that without overshooting into a standard the role doesn't need.

What to test

  • Functional strength — a genuine benchmark this time, not just standing tolerance: the ability to physically hold a position against a pushing crowd or an attempted forced entry for the short window before backup (the second guard, police, or both) arrives. This is a real, job-relevant capability now, not an arbitrary fitness signal
  • Endurance and agility — comfortably managing the building's stairs and tower under exertion, not just at a walking pace, plus a basic timed mobility circuit
  • Vision — corrected-to-normal, for CCTV monitoring and general observation
  • Scenario response, now including a physical component — de-escalation is still the first move for anything short of a genuine breach attempt, matching Document 12's escalation ladder; but the assessment should also test a simulated crowd/forced-entry scenario, not only the "visitor needs help" prompt from the original draft
  • Prior crowd-control or civil-disturbance experience — genuinely weighted in scoring now, not just a nice-to-have; this is exactly what ex-servicemen and police-veteran candidates (already the top recruitment channel below) bring that a purely hospitality-background candidate doesn't

What stays out, and why the line is drawn there

  • No firearms, and no arms-license pursuit — that's a different, much heavier compliance regime (Arms Act 1959, tied to demonstrated need like cash-in-transit or VIP protection) that doesn't fit this campus and would raise the risk profile rather than lower it. A visibly armed guard at a library-and-café campus reads as alarming, not reassuring, to the actual visitor base
  • Still no explicit age or body-type cutoff as a hard bar — the assessment tests function (can this person perform the scenario), which is both the fairer standard and, now, the more genuinely demanding one; a candidate who passes the functional strength and scenario tests qualifies regardless of age or build
  • Run by the Campus Director with Anna's sign-off given the elevated stakes — a step up from the original draft's "Campus Director only," matching how Document 06 already reserves the highest-stakes calls for the founder

Worth a real decision, not a silent one: is two guards still enough? With rotating shifts, two direct-hire guards likely means only one is on-site at any given moment. If the elevated posture above reflects a genuine ongoing concern rather than a one-time calibration, it's worth weighing a third hire against the ₹16,000–20,000/month figure already set in Document 06 — roughly ₹1.9–2.4L/year fully loaded — purely so the property isn't relying on a single guard during exactly the kind of incident this section now plans for. This is a headcount and budget decision for Anna to make deliberately, not something this document changes on its own.

Training — replacing what the agency's 160 hours used to cover

This is the one place a direct hire genuinely cannot just skip what an agency provided. PSARA mandates a minimum 160 hours of training for agency-employed guards, covering basic security concepts, physical fitness, emergency response, and self-defence. SCRIPTORIUM isn't bound by that mandate directly — but the underlying skill gap doesn't disappear just because the legal requirement doesn't apply, so the practical move is to replicate it voluntarily. Given the revision above, add a fourth module: crowd management and civil-disturbance response specifically, ideally from the same certified institute delivering the core course rather than improvised in-house.

Recruitment, unchanged but now more clearly weighted: Section 03's existing preference for ex-servicemen and police-veteran candidates was already the right channel — this revision is a reason to actually prioritise it in scoring, not just list it as one option among several. A candidate with genuine crowd-control or civil-disturbance experience from prior service is now solving a real requirement, not just a nice-to-have credential.

04 Emergency response — the short version

Full protocols belong in an actual staff training manual once construction is closer to complete — this is the campus-wide skeleton every zone's senior role and Security should know cold.

Fire

  • Nearest zone staff triggers the alarm and begins directing visitors to the Central Atrium Spine
  • Security sweeps their assigned zone for anyone who hasn't moved, then proceeds to the frontyard assembly point
  • Campus Director or senior-most person present confirms headcount against that day's staff roster

Medical emergency

  • Nearest staff member stays with the person; Security is radioed and brings the nearest first-aid kit
  • Campus Director makes the call on when to involve an ambulance — err toward calling early rather than waiting to see if it resolves

Power outage

  • Security checks the Studio server rack and the Capsule pods first — both have equipment and people that a sudden outage affects most directly
  • Emergency/exit lighting should be on a separate backup circuit — confirm this is part of the electrical brief, not assumed

Lost child or vulnerable visitor

  • Radio all staff immediately with a description; Security checks exits first, since that's the most time-sensitive risk
  • Never release a child to anyone without matching them to the adult they arrived with, confirmed by whichever staff member first noticed them
