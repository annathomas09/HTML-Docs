01 · The Name

Why Scriptorium

A scriptorium was the dedicated room in every medieval monastery where manuscript knowledge was copied, illuminated, preserved, and transmitted across generations. Every text that survived the Dark Ages — every early Islamic philosophical treatise, every Greek scientific work, every Indian mathematical advance — passed through a scriptorium before it reached the world that came after. The name is not metaphorical. SCRIPTORIUM is what this campus actually is: a room where knowledge is held, worked with, and made, in every form that knowledge currently takes — printed books available to anyone who walks in, soundproofed study capsules for sustained private work, a stationery tower for the physical practice of writing and craft, and a professional digital studio for the work that requires a screen.

The word is Latin, which means it carries no regional, linguistic, or community associations within India. It belongs to no state, no language family, no tradition that would make it feel local to one city and foreign in another. SCRIPTORIUM, Koramangala and SCRIPTORIUM, Connaught Place read identically — each location is its own institution, its own landmark, its own reason to visit.

The Arch Device

The arch device is a single pointed Gothic arch drawn in one unbroken weight — no fill, no shadow, no ornament. It is the architectural form that defines the building's structural language, reduced to its essential geometry. It appears above the wordmark in the full lockup, and functions as a stand-alone mark on capsule pod doors, membership cards, and stationery packaging where the full wordmark is impractical.

Mark construction rules

Never do

Do not add a dropshadow, glow, or emboss effect to the wordmark in digital applications. The only permitted depth effect is physical — stone relief carving, blind embossing on the membership card. On screen the wordmark is always flat.

Always do

The wordmark is always set in EB Garamond small-caps at a minimum of 0.14em letter-spacing. Never set it in any other typeface. Never set it in mixed case (Scriptorium) in formal applications — only in small-caps (SCRIPTORIUM).

Never do

Do not abbreviate the name to "Scrip," "Script," or any short form in any external-facing communication — signage, social handles, packaging, or digital interfaces. The name is always given in full.

Always do

Maintain clear space equal to the cap-height of the wordmark on all four sides of the full lockup. The arch device, when used alone, requires clear space equal to its own height on all sides.

03 · Typeface System

Three Typefaces, Three Roles

SCRIPTORIUM uses three typefaces. Each has a single, non-negotiable role. No other typefaces appear in any SCRIPTORIUM-branded communication, physical or digital.

EB Garamond Display · Wordmark · All headings · Zone names · Body text in print
Scriptorium
The act of reading is itself a form of making.
Use for: The wordmark. All headings at every level. Body text in printed materials (membership welcome letters, workshop invitations, the library's borrowing slips). Zone name labels in small-caps. Pull quotes and any text that should feel institutional.
Source: Google Fonts (EB Garamond) — free, open-source, correct for both digital and professional print. For professional print, pair with Adobe Garamond Premier Pro for the tightest possible small-caps rendering.
Why this face: Claude Garamond cut this typeface in Paris in the 1530s for scholarly printing. It was the typeface of the monastery scriptorium tradition translated into metal type. It has been used by university presses, the Bodleian Library, and scholarly publishers for five hundred years. There is no other choice for SCRIPTORIUM.
Gill Sans Sub-brand zone labels · Wayfinding · Interface buttons · Short operational text
Reads · Capsule · Ink · Studio
Use for: The zone name that follows the SCRIPTORIUM parent wordmark in sub-brand lockups (e.g., SCRIPTORIUM in Garamond small-caps, then READS in Gill Sans). Interior wayfinding plaques at distance. Button labels in the booking interface. Any short operational label that must be legible at a glance from more than 2 metres away.
Why this face: Eric Gill designed this typeface in 1928 explicitly for stone inscription — its letterforms are derived from his work as a monumental letter-cutter. It is the only widely available sans-serif typeface with a genuine architectural lineage, which makes it the correct complement to Garamond in a Gothic building context. On Windows systems without Gill Sans MT installed, substitute Calibri. On macOS, Gill Sans is system-native.
IBM Plex Mono All data · Colour codes · Measurements · Booking interfaces · Capsule numbering · Technical labels
#1C1A18 · POD 07 · 09:00–13:00
Use for: Colour hex codes and Pantone references in this document and any production specification. Capsule pod numbers on frosted panel doors. Time slots and booking confirmations in the digital interface. Technical specifications on signage production drawings. Any context where precision and legibility of data matters more than personality.
Why this face: IBM Plex Mono is the only typeface in the system that signals "now" rather than "then" — it reads as contemporary technology and precision in a way that creates a deliberate contrast with Garamond. In SCRIPTORIUM Capsule and SCRIPTORIUM Studio, this contrast between the ancient and the precise is exactly the emotional register to maintain.

05 · Sub-brand System

Four Zones, One Parent

SCRIPTORIUM is the parent. Each zone carries the parent name first — in Garamond small-caps — followed by the zone name in Gill Sans, in that zone's accent colour. The parent name never changes weight or typeface between zones. The zone name is always smaller than the parent name. The hierarchy is always parent before zone, never the reverse.

07 · Brand Voice

How SCRIPTORIUM Speaks

The SCRIPTORIUM voice is the voice of a very good librarian: unhurried, precise, warm without being casual. It does not sell. It describes. It trusts the person it is speaking to. It does not use exclamation marks in any formal context, does not use marketing language, and never tells a visitor what to feel. It says what is there, how it works, and where to find it — and leaves the rest to the building.

Always write in complete sentences.
Not "Free. Open. Yours." — that is a branding cliché. Not "Open to all visitors, featuring a wide variety of books for reading." — that is corporate padding. The correct register is the middle: "SCRIPTORIUM Reads is open to anyone who walks in. No card, no fee, no registration."
Never use the word "community."
Or: hub, innovative, smart, world-class, curated, ecosystem, journey, experience (as a noun replacing "visit"), or any portmanteau word ending in "-preneur." These words are the anti-SCRIPTORIUM. The brand's register is older than these words, and it should read that way.
Headlines are always in small-capitals.
Matching the register of the wordmark. Sub-headings and body text are in sentence case. All-caps is never used for emphasis — emphasis is achieved through sentence construction, not capitalisation.
On the open library
✗ "Explore our amazing collection of books in a stunning Gothic space!"
✓ "SCRIPTORIUM Reads holds books across general fiction, Indian and world history, science, and competitive examination reference. It is open without charge to anyone who enters."
On booking a capsule
✗ "Book your productivity pod now and unlock your full potential!"
✓ "Capsules are available by the hour or by the month. Each includes a desk, a small selection of books on loan, and a frosted panel you can close."
On A&M Munchies (referring visitors)
✗ "Fuel your study session with delicious drinks from our on-site café partner!"
✓ "A&M Munchies is at the end of the atrium passage, on the right as you face the garden."
On the stationery tower
✗ "Three floors of premium stationery. Find your next favourite pen!"
✓ "SCRIPTORIUM Ink occupies three floors. The ground floor is given over to writing instruments and inks; the first to paper, notebooks, correspondence, and — new as of this revision — a school and college book counter; the second to drawing, painting, and craft."

The book counter (Document 09, Section 09) is deliberately positioned as an Ink product line, not a SCRIPTORIUM Reads extension — Reads' voice and curation principles are untouched by it. When describing Ink, it's fair to call it a "one-stop destination for students," but that phrase should never attach itself to Reads, whose whole identity in Document 09 is built on being the opposite of transactional.

08 · Brand Ecosystem

Four Brands, One Founder

SCRIPTORIUM sits within a wider institutional universe. The four entities share one founder but operate as distinct brands with distinct visual identities, distinct scopes, and distinct purposes. They are related the way a great library, a university, an accreditation board, and a restaurant are related — by geography and ownership, not by name or logo.

EGEB Equitas Global Education Board Worldwide
The governing and accreditation body. Its visual identity should feel regulatory and institutional — formal, authoritative, probably darker and more structured than SCRIPTORIUM. No visual overlap with SCRIPTORIUM. EGEB does not use the arch device, the Parchment colour, or Garamond. It is a different institution entirely.
EGEB governs academic standards. It does not operate physical spaces.
TAU Thomas Aldenmere University City · Regional
The degree-granting university. Located near but institutionally separate from SCRIPTORIUM. TAU has its own identity rooted in its founder's name — a classical scholarly weight that is distinct from but compatible with SCRIPTORIUM's. TAU students receive paid internships across all SCRIPTORIUM zones. This relationship is operational, not visual — no co-branding, no shared logos, no joint signage.
TAU produces graduates. Some of them intern at SCRIPTORIUM. The relationship is a fact, not a marketing position.
SCRIPTORIUM The Study Campus Nationwide
This document. The physical campus chain. SCRIPTORIUM is the public face of the founder's institutional ecosystem — the thing most people encounter first, the thing that earns the revenue that funds the rest, the thing that puts the name on the street. Its identity is defined entirely in this document.
Each SCRIPTORIUM campus contains one A&M Munchies location. The two brands are tenants of the same building, not sub-brands of the same identity.
A&M A&M Munchies International
The founder's café brand. International trajectory. Its own identity: the name carries the founder's initials and a warm, personal, non-institutional word. A&M Munchies is the warmest brand in the ecosystem — the most human, the most personal, the most food-forward. Its identity should feel the way good food feels: generous, unpretentious, particular. To be developed separately from this document.
09 · Trademark Priority

Filing Sequence

File these in order. Each application is independent. Failing to file in Class 16 before the stationery tower opens is the most common and most expensive oversight in multi-zone brand building — a third party filing SCRIPTORIUM in Class 16 before you do would have a legitimate claim to the name for stationery products sold in India.

  • Class 41 · Education & Training Services. Covers the study capsule bookings, cyber café services, and any workshop or event run from SCRIPTORIUM premises. File immediately — this is the primary commercial class. The campus cannot operate commercially without this protection.
  • Class 35 · Retail Services. Covers the stationery retail operation across all three floors. File before the stationery tower opens. Required to protect the SCRIPTORIUM Ink sub-brand as a retail identity.
  • Class 16 · Paper Goods & Stationery. Covers SCRIPTORIUM-branded stationery products sold at the Ink counter — branded notebooks, packaging, pen-wrapping paper. File alongside Class 35.
  • Class 43 · Food & Beverage Services. Note: A&M Munchies will file separately in this class for the café. However, if SCRIPTORIUM intends to ever operate its own café concept under the SCRIPTORIUM name (not A&M Munchies) in a future campus, file this class now as a defensive registration.
  • Class 42 · Technology & IT Services. Covers SCRIPTORIUM Studio (the cyber café) as a paid technology access service. File before the Studio opens to the public.
  • Device mark — the arch device. File the arch device as a separate figurative trade mark in Classes 41, 35, and 16. A word mark and a device mark are different registrations; owning the word SCRIPTORIUM does not automatically protect the arch device, and vice versa.
Search first

Before filing any application, run a search on ipindiaonline.gov.in/tmrpublicsearch for SCRIPTORIUM in each class. If any conflicting mark is found, consult a trademark attorney before proceeding — a cease-and-desist from a prior registrant after you have built and opened a campus is a project-level risk, not just a legal inconvenience.
