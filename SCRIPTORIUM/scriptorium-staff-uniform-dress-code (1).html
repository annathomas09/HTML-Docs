SCRIPTORIUM — What Everyone Wears, and Why

Two uniform systems, one campus: SCRIPTORIUM's five zones and A&M Munchies dressed as the genuinely separate identities Document 04 says they are · Said-ul-Ajaib, South Delhi · July 2026

Founder: Anna Thomas Staff covered: 19 roles, Document 06 §11 Systems: 2 — SCRIPTORIUM zones · A&M Munchies Governing doc: Brand Identity, Document 04

00 The one rule everything else follows

Document 04 (Brand Identity) already settled this, in language worth repeating in full here because it governs every decision in this document: "A&M Munchies is not a SCRIPTORIUM sub-brand. It is a separate international café identity with its own visual language, its own colours, and its own expansion path... No A&M Munchies branded element should incorporate the Garamond wordmark, the arch device, or any colour from the SCRIPTORIUM palette." That rule applies to uniforms exactly as it applies to signage. So this document is really two uniform systems wearing one badge of convenience — a campus ID — and nothing else in common.

The practical consequence: if you take one thing from this document, take this — never let a single vendor, a single fabric order, or a single "campus uniform" conversation collapse the two systems into one. SCRIPTORIUM zone staff (Reads, Capsule, Ink, Studio, management) dress in the dark-academia language the building itself speaks. A&M Munchies staff dress in their own café identity, in their own palette, sourced and briefed separately. A vendor who doesn't understand why is the wrong vendor.

System A — SCRIPTORIUM zones

  • Covers: Campus Director, Head Librarian, Library Assistant/Capsule Host, Ink Floor Manager & Buyer, Floor Associates, Studio Manager, Studio Technician — 10 people
  • Palette: Scriptorium Black, Kota Grey, Sandstone, Parchment, Brass hardware (Document 04's five primary colours)
  • Identity: dark academia — the Gothic collegiate building, worn

System B — A&M Munchies

  • Covers: Head Barista & Café Manager, Junior Barista, Kitchen/Food Prep, Cashier/Service Staff — 4 people
  • Palette: its own — warm cream, taupe, chocolate brown (proposed below; no SCRIPTORIUM hex appears here by rule)
  • Identity: international café/patisserie — independent of the Gothic language entirely

Security and Maintenance/Housekeeping sit outside both systems — they're covered separately in Sections 05–06, since neither is a customer-facing sub-brand role in Document 04's sense.

01 System A — the shared foundation

Every SCRIPTORIUM-zone role starts from the same base wardrobe before any zone-specific accent is added. This is deliberate: a visitor walking the Central Atrium Spine should be able to tell, at a glance, "that person works here," before they can tell which zone. The reference direction is the dark-academia silhouette in your own research — structured waistcoat, white or ecru shirt, tailored trouser, leather shoe — the same visual grammar as the Gothic building itself: dark, tailored, a little severe, warmed by brass.

The base pieces (all genders, same silhouette logic)

  • Shirt: white or ecru cotton, full sleeve, structured collar — the one piece every SCRIPTORIUM-zone role shares without variation
  • Waistcoat: single-breasted, dark (Scriptorium Black or a deep charcoal-brown reading close to Kota Grey), brass buttons — this is the piece your reference images (4, 7, 8) centre on, and it's the single most identity-carrying garment in the system
  • Trouser: wide-leg or tailored straight, dark brown or charcoal, no visible branding
  • Neckwear: a slim dark tie or ribbon-tie for customer-facing roles (Head Librarian, Campus Director, Floor Managers); optional for back-of-house/technical roles (Studio Technician)
  • Footwear: black or dark brown leather shoe or boot — closed, low heel, quiet on Kota stone floors

Where the reference images map

  • Image 4 (six mannequin outfits): the governing silhouette — waistcoat-over-shirt, circle skirt or tailored trouser, boots. This is the base wardrobe language, not a literal costume; SCRIPTORIUM staff wear a working version of it, not the styled photoshoot version
  • Image 7 (glasses, shirt, wide trouser, annotated book): the "scholar" register — useful specifically for the Head Librarian and Ink Floor Manager, roles where looking like someone who actually reads/writes matters to the role itself
  • Image 8 (argyle sweater vest, tie, bookstore): a cooler-weather layer option — the argyle vest can replace the waistcoat November–February when Delhi is genuinely cold, without breaking the silhouette

Gender-neutral by design: the waistcoat-shirt-trouser silhouette is specified identically for every gender — no separate "female version" with a skirt default. Anyone who prefers the circle-skirt styling from Image 4 in place of trousers may — it's offered as an option within the same silhouette, not assigned by gender. This matches the same no-assumptions approach already established in Document 06's benefits policy.

02 System A — zone by zone

Each zone adds exactly one thing to the shared foundation: a colour accent matching its Document 04 sub-brand hue, applied in the smallest possible dose — a pin, a thread, a lining. Document 04 is explicit that zone accent colours are for "wayfinding plaques" and "sub-brand contexts," used in small doses, never as a wholesale costume change. Uniforms follow that same restraint.

03 The brass pin — one badge, five ribbons

Document 04 already anticipates exactly this use case: the arch device alone, in brass, is specified for "minimal contexts" where the full wordmark is impractical — a staff uniform is precisely that context. One small enamel or embroidered pin, worn on the left side of the waistcoat, does the entire job of identifying "this person works at SCRIPTORIUM" without printing the wordmark on fabric at all.

The pin itself

  • Single pointed Gothic arch, brass finish, no fill — identical geometry to the "device only · brass · minimal contexts" mark in Document 04
  • ~18–22mm — small enough to read as jewellery, not a corporate badge
  • Same pin for every SCRIPTORIUM-zone role; the ribbon behind it carries the zone colour, not the pin itself

Why a ribbon, not a coloured pin

  • Keeps the brass consistent — a gold-on-gold pin in five different accent metals would look like five different vendors, not one campus
  • A ribbon or thread is cheap to produce in five colours and cheap to replace if a zone's role changes — the pin itself is the durable, reordered-rarely piece
  • Reads correctly at conversational distance (close enough to greet a visitor) without needing wayfinding-plaque-scale colour blocking

05 Security — two directions, now a simple choice

Resolved since the last draft of this document: SCRIPTORIUM now hires security staff directly rather than through an agency (Document 06, Section 08) — one of the reasons for the change was exactly this uniform question. A directly-employed guard wears whatever SCRIPTORIUM commissions, the same way every other role in this document does. There's no agency contract to negotiate around; Direction A below is simply an ordering decision now, not a negotiation.

Direction A — Private security register (Image 5)

  • Black windbreaker/jacket with "SECURITY" in reflective or white text across the chest, black cap (same text), aviator sunglasses, earpiece, tie
  • Reads as premium private security — hotel, embassy, high-end retail. Matches SCRIPTORIUM's positioning as a premium campus better than a generic mall-guard look
  • Less weather-adaptable — the windbreaker is a single layer; needs a separate summer version for Delhi's April–June heat

Direction B — Structured tactical register (Image 6)

  • Cargo-style uniform shirt and trousers, shoulder patches/badges, belt, combat-style boots, cap
  • Reads as more visibly "on duty" and procedural — better if the priority is visible deterrence over premium-hospitality signalling
  • More layers and pockets make it more adaptable across seasons, but it's a harder visual match to a Gothic-collegiate/K-drama-café campus aesthetic — this is the one uniform in the whole document that doesn't need to match the building

Recommendation: Direction A — it matches the "quality across every touchpoint" standard the rest of this campus is held to, and there's no longer an agency constraint standing in the way of it. Security is often the very first staff member a visitor encounters at the gate, so the first impression carries real weight. Budget it the same way every other role's uniform is budgeted in Section 11 below, rather than as an agency pass-through cost.

The recruitment, physical-assessment, and training process behind these two people — now that SCRIPTORIUM runs it directly — is covered in Document 13 (Safety & Security), not here; this section stays focused on what they wear.

06 Maintenance & Housekeeping

No reference images covered this role, so this is proposed from function rather than styled from inspiration — which is the right way to build it anyway, since these two people move through every zone doing physically demanding, often unglamorous work all day.

The uniform

  • Utility shirt + trouser set in Kota Grey — durable poly-cotton, not the tailored fabric used elsewhere, since this role needs to move, kneel, and carry all day
  • No waistcoat, no tie — pure function
  • Small embroidered arch device on the chest (same brass mark as System A, no accent ribbon) — enough to identify as SCRIPTORIUM staff without any garment that would restrict movement

Practical notes

  • Provide 3 sets rather than the standard 2 (Section 07) — this role is hardest on fabric and most likely to need a same-day change after a spill or repair job
  • Include a weather layer: a lightweight rain shell for monsoon garden/pond maintenance work (cross-references Document 10's seasonal maintenance calendar)

08 Universal dress code — grooming and presentation

Kept short and function-driven on purpose. The uniform already does the work of looking like SCRIPTORIUM; the grooming code exists to protect hygiene and safety, not to police personal appearance beyond that.

Required

  • Uniform clean and pressed at the start of every shift — visible wrinkles or stains are a same-day fix, not a next-shift one
  • Hair restrained/tied back for any food-adjacent or kitchen role (Section 04) — this is a hygiene rule, not a style rule, and applies regardless of length or gender
  • Closed-toe shoes for Kitchen/Food Prep, Maintenance, and Security — safety-driven, non-negotiable for these three roles specifically
  • Name badge or pin visible and facing forward

Not permitted

  • Personal outerwear (hoodies, jackets not part of the uniform system) worn over the uniform on the floor during visitor hours
  • Strong fragrance in the Reads or Ink zones specifically — both are quiet, close-proximity browsing environments where scent carries further than in the café or Studio
  • Visible SCRIPTORIUM uniform pieces worn off-site in a way that implies an official campus appearance (a media interview, a personal social post presented as if speaking for SCRIPTORIUM) without the Campus Director's sign-off — this mirrors the brand-voice control already established in Document 04

09 Religious and cultural accommodation

This section exists because it needs to be explicit, not assumed. SCRIPTORIUM's staff policies already commit to festival bonuses regardless of religion and to inclusive policies that require no personal disclosure (Document 06). The uniform system extends the same principle: nothing above is designed to be worn instead of religious or cultural dress — it's designed to be worn with it.

Explicitly accommodated, no approval needed

  • Hijab, in the zone's accent colour or a neutral matching the base wardrobe (black, charcoal, cream) — treated as a uniform piece, not an exception to one
  • Turban/pagri, worn as the staff member's own choice of colour
  • Bindi, tilak, mangalsutra, kara, cross pendant, or other religious jewellery worn discreetly with the uniform
  • Beard length or style kept or grown for religious reasons — exempt from any grooming expectation elsewhere in this document

How this is handled operationally

  • No staff member is asked to state a religious reason to receive an accommodation — the accommodation is available on request, full stop, matching the "no personal disclosure" standard already set elsewhere
  • Prayer breaks are scheduled around, not instead of, floor coverage — the Campus Director builds this into shift planning the same way meal breaks already are
  • Kitchen/Food Prep hijab or turban options use food-safe fabric and a secure style compatible with the hair-restraint requirement in Section 08 — worth specifying with the uniform vendor directly rather than treating it as a bolt-on

Laundering

  • Kitchen and Maintenance uniforms: campus-laundered, not staff-laundered — food-safety and hygiene standards are hard to guarantee on a home wash cycle, and this is standard practice at any serious F&B operation
  • System A base wardrobe (waistcoat, shirt, trouser): staff-laundered is acceptable given the lower soil/spill exposure, with a care-instruction card issued alongside the uniform
  • A&M Munchies front-of-house whites: campus-laundered if budget allows — white shows soil fastest and represents the café's first impression

Replacement policy

  • Standard replacement cycle: 12 months for base wardrobe pieces, 6 months for kitchen aprons/coats (higher wear, more frequent hot-water laundering)
  • Damage beyond repair (not normal wear) replaced immediately, at campus cost, no questions asked — this is the same "quality over convenience" standard the rest of Document 06 holds staffing decisions to
