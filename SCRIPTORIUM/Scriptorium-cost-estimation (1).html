Location Why Said-ul-Ajaib is a strong choice

Said-ul-Ajaib is an urban village in South Delhi that sits in a genuinely rare sweet spot: close enough to Saket's infrastructure and footfall to benefit from it, but still priced at village-land rates significantly below Saket proper. Its proximity to multiple metro stations and — critically — the Garden of Five Senses directly adjacent makes it perhaps the best possible site for a campus with a pond and landscape ambition.

The strategic logic: UPSC, DU, IIT-Delhi, and IGNOU students all converge on the Saket–Qutab Minar metro belt. Self-study paid libraries in this corridor charge ₹1,600–3,500/month per seat and are consistently full. Said-ul-Ajaib sits at the centre of this demand, with land at roughly one-third the cost of Saket proper — the gap between what you pay for land and what your users are willing to pay for quality study space is where the business case lives.

Critical zoning check before shortlisting any specific plot: Urban village land in Delhi (lal dora) has complex title and zoning issues. Confirm three things with a property lawyer before making any offer: (1) whether the plot falls on a notified mixed-use street or is within a local shopping centre zone under MPD-2021, (2) whether the title is clear and the plot is not sub-judice, and (3) whether conversion from village use to commercial use has been regularised or needs DDA/MCD sanction. Lal dora plots cannot be registered under normal Delhi land records — they use a different legal regime. Get a specialist, not a general property broker, to advise on this.

Structure Three property scenarios — pick your entry point

The biggest single variable in this project's capital requirement is whether you own the land, lease a built space, or build in phases on a smaller lease. Each has a fundamentally different risk profile and capital need.

Scenario A
Lease & Fit-Out
Lease a large existing commercial building near Saket Metro — ideally a bare-shell or warm-shell space of 8,000–12,000 sq ft — and fit it out to spec. Cheapest entry, fastest to open. The trade-off: you cannot build the library's double-height void or the stationery tower if they aren't already in the structure; your architectural ambition is capped by what the landlord built. Also, lease security risk — you're building ₹5–7 crore of fit-out in someone else's property.
Est. total: ₹8–12 crore + ₹40–65 lakh/month rent
Scenario B — Recommended for this vision
Own Plot, Build to Spec
Purchase a 6,000–8,000 sq ft plot in the Said-ul-Ajaib / Neb Sarai / Freedom Fighters Enclave village belt, then design and build from ground up. Only this scenario allows the Gothic library double-height void, the 3-floor stationery tower, the solar lantern roofline, and the backyard pond to be built exactly as designed. Highest capital requirement, but you own a South Delhi asset that appreciates. Long-term this is the strongest financial position.
Est. total: ₹22–32 crore all-in
Scenario C
Phased Start — Lease First, Build Later
Open the café + study library core (Phase 1 only) in a leased South Delhi space to prove the model and build revenue, then use operating profit + bank loan against the concept to purchase land and build the full campus in phases 3–5 years later. Significantly de-risks the capital commitment. The full architectural vision is deferred, not abandoned. Operationally sensible for a first-time operator.
Phase 1: ₹1.5–2.5 crore · Full build: Scenario B timeline

The rest of this document prices Scenario B in full — the complete, own-plot build. If Scenario C is the chosen path, use only the Phase 1 rows initially and treat the rest as a 5-year capital plan.

Note on circle rates: Commercial property circle rates in Delhi are calculated by multiplying the residential land rate by a factor (typically 1.5×–2×). Said-ul-Ajaib falls in category E–F for residential (₹46,200–₹92,400 per sq m). Always get three independent valuations before making an offer.

The mezzanine is the structural commitment: Delhi 2026 construction costs add 15–25% for double-height voids and complex cantilever spans. The library mezzanine ring must be sized for live load (people + bookshelves) and appears in the structural engineer's calculations at the very first design stage — it cannot be added later without prohibitive cost. Commission the structural engineer before finalising the building plan, not after.

On commercial solar subsidy: The PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana (₹78,000 subsidy per 3kW) applies to residential consumers only. For a commercial premises like this campus, you instead benefit from: (a) accelerated depreciation of 40% on solar plant in Year 1 under the Income Tax Act, reducing your effective investment by roughly ₹6–8 lakhs on a 25kW system, (b) net metering credits from BSES offsetting your monthly electricity bill, and (c) Delhi's Generation Based Incentive (GBI) of ₹2–3 per unit generated. Collectively these can reduce effective solar cost by 25–30% over the first three years — not as dramatic as the residential subsidy, but still strong economics.
